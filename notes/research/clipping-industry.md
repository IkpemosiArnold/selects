# Clipping tools label virality; none predict it

Research date: Thursday 22 October 2026. Synthesised from seven research notes in `notes/research/research_notes/AI clipping industry/`, the Selects README, `eval/PLAN.md`, `eval/results/RESULTS.md`, `notes/MODELS.md`, and Come Through's `eval/personas/FROZEN.md` and `eval/world.mjs`. Every external claim carries an inline source with its date, or "accessed 22 Oct 2026" where the page has no date. Vendor marketing and independent evidence are kept apart. UNVERIFIED marks anything that could not be confirmed against a primary source. Research limits are listed near the end.

## Executive summary

**No clipping product has shown that it predicts virality. They attach a rubric score that ranks clips within one upload, and none has published a test of that score against real views.** OpusClip, Klap, Quso, Vizard, StreamLadder and others ship a 0 to 99 or 0 to 100 "virality" number built from named dimensions such as hook, flow, value and trend. OpusClip's own developer blog tells users to "treat scores as ranking, not approval" and admits that "some 60-scored clips outperform 85-scored ones" ([OpusClip API blog, 13 May 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-moment-detection-api)). The academic literature puts a hard ceiling on any content-only predictor. The creator's own history and the platform's feedback loop explain most of a clip's reach, and within-video replay peaks are barely predictable from content ([Martin et al., WWW 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01013); [Duico et al., 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.06102)). Selects' pre-registered result (AUC 0.56, 95% CI 0.49 to 0.62, n = 19 videos) sits where that literature says a zero-shot transcript judge should sit ([Selects RESULTS.md, 21 Oct 2026](../../eval/results/RESULTS.md)). The one real asymmetry is data. OpusClip says it fine-tunes on its users' "likes, dislikes, exports, and social performance", and its privacy policy covers pulling performance analytics from connected social accounts ([OpusClip blog, Aug/Sep 2024](https://www.opus.pro/blog/opusclip-celebrates-30m-in-funding-and-the-launch-of-clipanything); [OpusClip privacy policy, version 17 Nov 2025](https://www.opus.pro/privacy-policy)). A free local tool cannot copy that pooled data, but it can build the part the literature says matters most: a per-creator loop that learns from that creator's own results on their own device.

**Q1, "Why can other clipping software predict virality but Selects can't?"** The premise is wrong: they cannot either. What they ship is a label. It is a prompted or lightly tuned judgement of hook, flow and payoff, and it ranks clips relative to each other ([OpusClip help, accessed 22 Oct 2026](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/virality-score)). Quso's score is clamped to 75 to 99, so every clip looks strong ([Quso help, accessed 22 Oct 2026](https://helpdesk.quso.ai/help/virality-scrore)). The only accuracy figure any vendor gives is OpusClip's "70+ beats the median about 75% of the time on talking-head content". A growth marketer wrote it, with no sample, method or definition of the median ([OpusClip API blog, 13 May 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-moment-detection-api)). The best independent-ish test, 30 clips run by a competitor, found high-score clips averaged about 2.3 times the views of low-score clips. In the same data, the best performer scored 64 and the worst scored 87 ([ScaleReach, 1 May 2026, updated 15 Oct 2026](https://www.scalereach.ai/blog/opus-clip-review)). In the literature, content-only models reach Spearman 0.3 to 0.55 on cross-video popularity, and adding the creator's past success roughly doubles the explained variance ([Khosla et al., WWW 2014](https://people.csail.mit.edu/khosla/papers/www2014_khosla.pdf); [M3TR, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15455v2)). A 2026 transcript study finds that a position-in-video baseline comes within 0.03 mAP of a fine-tuned transformer at predicting replay peaks (abstract only, tables UNVERIFIED) ([Arora and Arora, NeurIPS 2026 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/159082)). The defensible version for Selects has four parts. Call the score a ranking aid with a published number. Add the position-only baseline that eval v2 lacked. Learn per creator from that creator's own approvals and posted results, stored on their device. Test picks against simple alternatives in the field.

**Q2, "If we depend on transcripts, we can't clip unclear-audio or no-speech content."** That is true of Selects as built: the transcript quality gate stops a job before judging when whisper's words are unreliable ([Selects README, accessed 22 Oct 2026](../../README.md)). It is not true of clipping in general. The published multimodal systems are audio-first, and the audio layer is cheap. IBM's golf highlight system gave crowd cheer a fusion weight of 0.61, against 0.13 each for commentator tone, commentator words and player reaction ([Merler et al., arXiv 1707.07075, Jul 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.07075)). Warner Bros. Discovery placed 24 of 25 football events within one second of the human log using only mel-band crowd energy and a 3.6 to 4.1 kHz whistle band, with no custom ML ([AWS M&E blog, 28 May 2024](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/how-warner-bros-discovery-uses-audio-analysis-to-improve-data-accuracy-and-enrich-the-fan-experience)). Laughter, applause, cheering and music are AudioSet classes. Open taggers that cover them run in a browser: YAMNet has 3.7M parameters and takes 12.29 ms per second of audio on a Pixel 6 CPU through MediaPipe ([YAMNet README](https://github.com/tensorflow/models/blob/master/research/audioset/yamnet/README.md); [MediaPipe Audio Classifier, updated 9 Oct 2026](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/audio/audio_classifier)). For noisy speech, two independent studies found that ML denoising before Whisper made transcripts worse. MetricGAN+ enhancement raised error in all 40 configurations tested, and SAM-Audio raised it too ([arXiv 2512.17562, Dec 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17562); [arXiv 2603.04710, Mar 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04710)). So when whisper's confidence collapses, Selects should switch to non-speech signals rather than stop or denoise. Gaming clippers already follow this pattern of probing the content and then choosing a signal set ([Medal support, Nov 2025](https://support.medal.tv/en/support/solutions/articles/48001167701)). No automatic router for general video has been published, so it is open ground.

**What to build first.** The top five roadmap items, ranked by impact over effort, all pass the zero-cost gate. (1) A position-only baseline plus honest score labelling. (2) Routing unreliable-transcript and non-speech videos to an audio-event path (whisper's own non-speech tokens, plus YAMNet or EfficientAT for laughter, applause, cheering and music) instead of stopping. (3) An on-device "learn from your results" loop that calibrates the ranker per creator. (4) A noisy-audio policy: raw audio into whisper, measure `--clean-audio`, denoise only the exported clip. (5) A small human-rated Nigerian English, Pidgin, Yoruba and Hausa transcription benchmark that decides which Apache-licensed ASR to route to. Each item comes with a pre-registered eval v3 test, set out below, that reuses `eval/` and Come Through's frozen-world pattern.

## Q1: Vendors rank clips, and nobody has shown they predict views

### What vendors claim, and how they hedge it

The vendor scores share a family resemblance: named rubric dimensions, a 0 to 100 scale, and no published model, weights or training labels in any help center. OpusClip's four factors, Klap's "hook quality, edit pacing, topic relevance, caption keyword density", and Vizard's "hook strength, clear takeaway, story flow" look like what an LLM prompted with a checklist produces ([Klap, accessed 22 Oct 2026](https://klap.app/tools/viral-clip-generator); [Vizard blog, 13 Jul 2026](https://vizard.ai/blog/meet-vizard-model-v2-full-context-clips-that-the-algorithm-actually-rewards)). Selects' rubric (hook, stands alone, payoff, clarity) is the same family of method. The difference is that Selects publishes its prompt and its weights ([Selects README](../../README.md)).

The hedges in vendor copy matter more than the headline. OpusClip's help center frames the score as a way to compare clips "relative to others" ([OpusClip help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/virality-score)). Its API blog says "treat scores as ranking, not approval" and names timing, caption and platform context as reasons low scores win ([OpusClip API blog, 13 May 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-moment-detection-api)). None of those reasons is an input to the score, and a third-party review states the score is "computed from the clip in isolation", without the creator's audience or posting history ([Autoposting.ai, 5 Jul 2026](https://autoposting.ai/blog/opus-clip-review)). OpusClip describes its training data three different ways: tens of thousands of viral videos (2024), hundreds of thousands of clip-performance pairs (2026 body text), and thousands of viral clips against millions of average ones (2026 FAQ). It gives scales of 0 to 99 and 0 to 100. The free tier hides the score entirely ([opus.pro/pricing](https://www.opus.pro/pricing)). Quso's 75 to 99 clamp and Opus's paywall suggest the score works partly as a conversion and confidence device. That is an inference, but it fits the clamp.

### The literature sets a low ceiling for content-only prediction

Ten years of popularity research agree that content alone explains little of reach. On 2.3M Flickr images, content features reached Spearman 0.31 against 0.77 for social features, and one feature, the user's mean views, alone reached 0.75 ([Khosla et al., WWW 2014](https://people.csail.mit.edu/khosla/papers/www2014_khosla.pdf)). On Twitter cascades, content-only R² was "negligibly low", below 0.05, while the poster's past success brought it to 0.42 to 0.48 ([Martin et al., WWW 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01013)). On TikTok-style video, the 2025 SMP Challenge winner's error jumped from MAPE 0.175 to 0.301 when user-profile features were removed, but only to 0.182 when the video embedding was removed ([MVP, arXiv 2507.00950, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00950)). A June 2026 study of 9,654 brand TikToks used Gemini 2.5 Pro to annotate 77 content variables. Those variables added only R² 0.056 to 0.078 beyond follower count and video age ([Paekivi and Karjus, arXiv 2606.16053, summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.16053)). The best retrieval-augmented micro-video model reached Spearman 0.559 on MicroLens ([M3TR, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15455v2)).

Randomness is built into the outcome. In Salganik's artificial music market, the same song's success varied widely across independent "worlds" with social influence, so that "the best songs rarely did poorly, and the worst rarely did well, but any other result was possible" ([Salganik, Dodds and Watts, Science 2006](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/303s19/docs/salganik_dodds_watts.pdf)). On TikTok, an account's most popular video has 64 times the views of its average video, against 40 times on YouTube ([Guinaudeau et al., CCR 2022, via TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2022-12-tiktok-lowers-barriers-virality-tight.pdf)). Early views, by contrast, are highly predictive. Log-scale correlation with 30-day popularity is 0.92 for YouTube after about 5 days and 0.98 for Digg after 5 hours ([Szabo and Huberman, 2008](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0811.0405)).

Two results mark where content does work. When the target is normalised by exposure (watch percentage, or the share of viewers who stay past 5 seconds), a content-only model reached Spearman about 0.70 on roughly 90,000 Snapchat Spotlight videos. Transcripts added nothing there, while visual, audio and aesthetic features carried the gains ([Li et al., ECCV 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00289v1)). A fine-tuned VideoLLaMA2 that hears audio won the 2025 version of that challenge at SROCC 0.691, ahead of a fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL at 0.665 ([Sun et al., arXiv 2508.02516, Aug 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02516v2)). And per-creator modelling helps: within a single user's own photos, content-only Spearman (0.40) beat social-only (0.21) ([Khosla et al., 2014](https://people.csail.mit.edu/khosla/papers/www2014_khosla.pdf)).

Within-video replay peaks, the label Selects uses, are the hardest target of all. On YTMR500, the best visual model got precision@15 of 22% against 15% chance, and MTurk humans performed at chance (Krippendorff alpha -0.017) ([Duico et al., arXiv 2309.06102, 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.06102)). In the Mr. HiSum study, human majority vote agreed with the Most Replayed label 64.8% of the time. The authors flag a "self-fulfilling bias": once the heatmap appears, at 50,000 views, viewers replay the marked parts ([Mr. HiSum, NeurIPS 2023](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/7f880e3a325b06e3601af1384a653038-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf)). A brain-encoding model's predicted engagement curve had a position-controlled partial r of only +0.058 (CI -0.04 to 0.15) against replay heatmaps on 48 videos ([Sahu and Pandey, arXiv 2607.01400, 2026](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.01400)). Zero-shot LLM judging, which is what Selects does, sits at the weak end of every result. Vanilla prompting reached 59 to 62.5% on an easy balanced task of local hits against global hits. Adding 10 retrieved labelled examples lifted it to 71.5 to 72%, and adding a supervised signal lifted it to 85.5% ([Kayal et al., ACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.597.pdf)).

Read against this, Selects' AUC of 0.56 is not a broken pipeline. AUC 0.56 means a replayed window outranks a non-replayed one 56% of the time, which is the same order of lift as Duico's supervised models. Two details in eval v2 support the "weak, structural signal" reading. A 0.5B Qwen model scored 0.54 (CI 0.52 to 0.57), close to the 26B Gemma. And the shortlist, not the judge, caps the result: a perfect picker restricted to the 8-window shortlist would hit a replay peak in the top 3 for only 53% of videos, against 89% from 16 windows ([RESULTS.md, 21 Oct 2026](../../eval/results/RESULTS.md)). Eval v2 never tested a position-only baseline, the control that Arora and Arora say nearly matches a fine-tuned transformer ([NeurIPS 2026 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/159082)).

### The data moat is real, unverified, and unproven as an advantage

OpusClip is the only vendor with a stated claim, a plausible pipeline and the scale to run one. It says ClipAnything was fine-tuned on 6M users' social performance ([OpusClip blog, 2024](https://www.opus.pro/blog/opusclip-celebrates-30m-in-funding-and-the-launch-of-clipanything)). Its privacy policy covers collecting "users' videos posted publicly on their social media accounts and any associated statistics", including "internal performance analytics", to "improve creator-tool features & benchmark trends". EU and EEA users can opt out of AI research use by email ([OpusClip privacy policy, version 17 Nov 2025](https://www.opus.pro/privacy-policy)). Its job posts ask for ranking, personalisation, preference optimisation and "learning from user feedback" (search snippet, date UNVERIFIED) ([Employbl mirror](https://www.employbl.com/jobs/lead-ai-researcher-opus-clip-1696107)). Reap makes a smaller version of the same claim ([Reap Research, 29 Apr 2026](https://reap.video/reports/state-of-top-ai-video-clipping-tools-2026)). No source shows how outcome labels are built (time window, normalisation by account size, platform), and no source shows connected-account analytics actually flowing into training rather than dashboards. Segment-level "virality scores" from engagement on each portion of a video are old prior art held by a platform: Meta's 2017 patent family ([US20170187772A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20170187772A1/en)). No patents assigned to OpusClip, Quso or Munch were found.

The moat cannot be copied for free, because it needs OAuth access to millions of users' social accounts and paid GPU fine-tuning. Two facts limit how much it is worth. OpusClip holds the data but publishes no calibration evidence. And the literature says creator history and platform randomness swamp clip-intrinsic quality, which a pooled global model cannot see for a given creator anyway. The free substitute aims at the variable that matters: one creator's own outcomes, kept on that creator's machine.

### Creators review the billing, not the score

Across review platforms, the score is nearly absent from what users write. OpusClip holds 4.0 on Trustpilot from 509 reviews, with 20% one-star. The negative tail is about credits, expiry and cancellation ("16 emails to cancel and they're still trying to charge me", 26 Sep 2026), and the one Product Hunt reviewer who mentioned the score said he had not used it ([Trustpilot, accessed 22 Oct 2026](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/opus.pro); [Product Hunt, accessed 22 Oct 2026](https://www.producthunt.com/products/opus-clip/reviews)). Klap (3.9, 30% one-star) and Submagic show the same billing pattern ([Trustpilot Klap](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/klap.app); [Trustpilot Submagic](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/submagic.co)). Vizard's 4.7 on 2,522 reviews includes only 9 from the last 12 months, which suggests the score is stale ([Trustpilot Vizard, accessed 22 Oct 2026](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/vizard.ai)). Testers report usable-output rates near 60% as-is, with 20 to 40% discarded ([ScaleReach, 2026](https://www.scalereach.ai/blog/opus-clip-review); [BIGVU, 2 Jul 2026](https://bigvu.tv/blog/opus-clip-tested-2026-where-ai-wins-40-percent-discard/)). A podcaster on Reddit, quoted second-hand, put it plainly: "you didn't use the good parts" (UNVERIFIED, thread undated) ([eesel.ai, edited 27 Sep 2026](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/opusclip-reviews)). One small spot check found caption accuracy fell from about 95% on clean solo audio to about 88% on overlapping or accented speech ([ScaleReach, 2026](https://www.scalereach.ai/blog/opus-clip-review)).

### Verdict: they label; Selects can measure

Vendors label clips. Their scores rank candidates within one upload using rubric judgements, possibly nudged by pooled outcome data in OpusClip's case, and they are sold with predictive language that the vendors' own fine print walks back. No one has shown calibrated prediction of views, and the literature says content-only prediction of raw views tops out around Spearman 0.3 to 0.55 across videos. Within one video, it sits barely above chance.

An honest version of the feature for Selects has five parts. All of them are free, and all of them are things competitors do not do. First, call the number what it is ("rank within this video") and print the measured AUC beside it, with its interval. Second, beat or match a position-only baseline before claiming the model adds anything, and fold position into the ranker if it helps. Third, widen the recall stage, since eval v2's ceiling rose from 53% to 89% hit@3 when the shortlist grew from 8 to 16 ([RESULTS.md](../../eval/results/RESULTS.md)). Fourth, learn per creator, on their device, from what they approve, skip, post and see perform. Fifth, test picks in the field against a position pick and a random pick of the same length, judged on 7-day views and retention. That gives a creator a forecast they can audit, built from their own numbers, which no pooled score offers.

### A content-type router for Selects

The design below combines published pieces into a router. It is a synthesis, not a published system, and every threshold is a placeholder to be tuned on the eval v3 dev set.

**Stage 0, cheap probes over the whole file (CPU, about 1 Hz).** These are: whisper's own transcript trust (Selects already computes word confidence, low-confidence share and loop detection); AudioSet tags per second from YAMNet or EfficientAT (speech, music, singing, laughter, cheering, applause, crowd, whistle, gunshot, explosion); whisper's non-speech tokens such as `[laughter]` and `(applause)` ([ChuckleNet-Kage card, accessed 22 Oct 2026](https://huggingface.co/Hayasuki/ChuckleNet-Kage)); the loudness envelope and excursions above each speaker's baseline (Powder's "yell" idea); shot-cut rate from ffmpeg's scene filter; motion energy from frame differences; OCR text density in fixed regions (score bug, kill feed); and sidecar data if present (chat log, Most Replayed heatmap).

**Stage 1, soft type classification.** Rules come first, with an optional CLIP zero-shot check. The output is a distribution over types, not a single label, so mixed videos blend weights. Talking head: speech ratio above about 0.6 and good transcript trust. Comedy or reaction: speech plus frequent laughter tags or tokens. Sports: crowd and cheer tags plus a persistent score bug. Gaming: persistent HUD text plus high motion, or a chat sidecar. Music or performance: music or singing tags dominate. Vlog or B-roll: low speech ratio, little HUD text, a moderate cut rate.

**Stage 2, candidate proposal per type.** Talking head keeps the current transcript path. Comedy uses laughter onsets as punchline ends, with the start found at the previous sentence boundary. Sports uses crowd energy (IBM's 0.61 weight as a starting prior), whistle-band energy, commentator excitement from pitch and energy plus an excitement word list on the transcript (IBM used 60 terms), and score-bug OCR changes. Gaming uses loudness spikes, laughter and lag-shifted chat bursts (the lag value is unsourced and must be tuned). Music uses energy peaks and applause, with downbeats as boundaries. Vlog uses shot-level salience, faces, motion peaks and diversity.

**Stage 3, boundaries.** Snap to sentence ends, silence gaps, shot boundaries or downbeats, depending on type. Silence-aware cuts follow AWS's tennis replay pattern ([AWS M&E blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/stream-tennis-matches-through-aws-elemental-medialive-and-generate-real-time-replays-with-amazon-rekognition/)).

**Stage 4, ranking.** Fuse z-normalised signals linearly per type. Then optionally re-rank the shortlist with Gemma over a text dossier for each candidate: transcript excerpt, audio tags, OCR text, and captions of 3 to 5 frames from a local VLM where the device can run one. A cloud VLM is allowed only through the user's own free key, and it fails closed on 429 or quota errors. The Gemma 4 video cap of 60 seconds at 1 fps fits this windowed design ([Gemma 4 card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4)).

The most important change is Stage 0's use of transcript trust. Today an unreliable transcript stops the job. Under the router it lowers the transcript's weight and raises the audio and visual paths, so a Pidgin skit with laughter, a match with a roaring crowd, or a dance video with no words still yields candidates. Faithful-cut checks stay attached wherever there are words.

### Noisy audio: keep raw audio for whisper, clean only what people hear

Vendors position enhancement as output polish, not as an ASR front end. Descript's documented flow transcribes first and then applies Studio Sound ([Descript, accessed 22 Oct 2026](https://descript.com/tools/voice-enhancer)). Adobe's Enhance Speech v2 is a separate tool from its transcription ([Adobe Podcast, Feb 2025](https://podcast.adobe.com/en/guides/latest-updates)). The independent evidence says enhancement before ASR hurts. MetricGAN+ made semantic WER worse in all 40 configurations across four ASR systems including Whisper, by 1.1 to 46.6 points ([arXiv 2512.17562, Dec 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17562)). SAM-Audio preprocessing raised Whisper's WER and CER on Bengali YouTube and noisy English, and the damage grew with model size ([arXiv 2603.04710, Mar 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04710)). Modern ASR is already fairly noise-robust: Parakeet v3 goes from 6.34% WER clean to 7.12% at 10 dB SNR ([Parakeet v3 card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)).

Selects' `--clean-audio` flag applies band limits (below 80 Hz, above 7.6 kHz) and loudness levelling before whisper ([Selects README](../../README.md)). That is gentler than ML enhancement, and it has not been measured against raw audio. The policy should be: raw audio into whisper by default; `--clean-audio` kept only if eval v3 shows it is no worse; DeepFilterNet (MIT or Apache) applied to the rendered clip's audio track, which is what viewers hear. When whisper's trust drops, the router takes over. For music under speech, HTDemucs vocal isolation is the one case where separation might help, but at about 1.5 times real time and several GB of GPU memory it belongs in an optional desktop path ([demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)).

## The clipping economy pays about $1 per 1,000 views, and fraud shapes the rules

Clipping has become a paid marketing channel with heavy 2025 to 2026 press coverage. Whop Content Rewards is the self-serve centre of it. A brand posts a campaign with a rate per 1,000 views and a budget, and clippers post on their own TikTok, Reels, Shorts or X accounts and submit links. After the seller approves a submission, Whop pays automatically for counted views ([Whop docs, via search snippet](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/growth-marketing/content-rewards)). A snapshot of all 50 live campaigns on 28 Sep 2026 found a **median rate of $1.00 per 1,000 views** (range $0.05 to $3.00). About $1.10M was budgeted and $784K spent, and Whop took a 10% fee. Per-clip minimums mean a clip at a $3 rate needs about 2,000 views before it enters review, and caps stop earnings at a fixed amount ([Foundry, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/what-is-clipping-1-dollar-per-1000-views-50-campaigns)). Forbes, reported second-hand, put Content Rewards payouts at over $40,000 a day across nearly a million videos a month in April 2026 ([CreatorDB, Aug 2026](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/)). A third-party tracker counted about $2.58M paid to 8,466 earners, roughly $305 each over their lifetime (UNVERIFIED, and likely a subset) ([Ascynd](https://ascynd.io/en/blog/whop-clipping)). Whop raised $200M from Tether at a $1.6B valuation in February 2026 ([Wikipedia: Whop.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whop.com)).

Rates outside Whop span a wider band. NPR reported 50 cents to $25 per 1,000 views depending on sponsor ([NPR, 12 May 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/12/nx-s1-5794670-e1/the-clipping-economy-how-short-form-video-clippers-are-overrunningthe-internet)). MrBeast paid the agency Clipping $50 per 100,000 views ($0.50 per 1,000), and Clipping reportedly earned about $7.7M with over 20,000 contracted clippers in about 10 months (Bloomberg, via Digiday) ([Digiday, 18 May 2026](https://digiday.com/media/the-case-for-and-against-clipping/)). One operator runs a network of about 40,000 freelance clippers, and "elite clippers" are offered $500 to $1,500 monthly retainers ([Moneywise via Yahoo Finance, 12 Jun 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/articles/clipping-side-hustle-youve-never-110000379.html)). The Kick streamer N3on reportedly paid $1.4M to 303 clippers in five weeks at $40 to $50 per 100,000 views. About half of his roughly 1,000-person network was recruited through a group built with Adin Ross across India and Nigeria ([CreatorDB, Aug 2026](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/)). Selects' own README cites a $0.40 per 1,000 views gig seen on r/naijaremote, which it marks unverified ([Selects README](../../README.md)).

View verification is where the economy is most fragile. Content Rewards' founder told Forbes that bot fraud is "the single biggest threat". The industry's answers include API-verified view counts pulled from platforms rather than screenshots, multi-layer bot detection, buyer-side conversion tracking, and geofencing "known view-farm regions", with one platform naming Pakistan, India and Indonesia ([TechBuzz, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/clipping-s-perfect-fraud-flywheel)). Whop counts only "legitimate views", excluding bots and paid inducements ([Foundry, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/what-is-clipping-1-dollar-per-1000-views-50-campaigns)). OpusClip describes an AI flagging step and a review window of about 72 hours, and notes view counts clustering near payout caps ([OpusClip blog, Aug 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards)). Some campaigns require that at least 50% of a clipper's audience be in the US ([Digiday, 18 May 2026](https://digiday.com/media/the-case-for-and-against-clipping/)). The method Whop uses to verify views could not be confirmed from a primary Whop page. Policy pressure is rising too. Instagram's Adam Mosseri said on 30 Apr 2026 that original creators should not "compete with accounts that simply re-upload" ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/the-case-for-and-against-clipping/)). Press reports cover faked organic virality, and a WSJ analysis found Polymarket clippers falsely depicting about $1.9M in winnings (via secondary summaries) ([Art of the Brand, 26 Sep 2026](https://artofthebrand.substack.com/p/how-clipping-became-a-billion-dollar)). Whop campaigns reject duplicate-looking clips ([OpenClip guide](https://openclip.app/guides/whop-clipping-guide)), which matters when many clippers run the same source through the same AI.

The arithmetic explains the demand for a free tool. At the $1.00 median less a 10% fee, an OpusClip Starter plan at $15 a month costs about the first 16,700 views a clipper earns each month, and a beginner's lifetime Whop earnings may be around $305. Speed and volume decide income, so automated moment-finding has real value. The reputational risk also points Selects' positioning toward creators clipping their own long-form work, and toward clippers working under a campaign's explicit rights, which matches its existing rights-and-attribution flow ([Selects README](../../README.md)). One further inference: per-creator calibration naturally produces different picks for different users, which reduces the duplicate-clip problem that shared AI tools create.

Nigerian YouTube content draws over 20B annual views and earned over $10M in AdSense in 2024 ([TechCabal, 25 Sep 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/25/at-31-2m-today-nigerias-creator-economy-could-be-worth-billions-by-2030/)). TikTok's Creator Rewards does not include Nigeria. Meta opened monetisation to Nigerian creators in June 2024, and TikTok reaches 47.8M Nigerian adults against 30.5M for YouTube ([Vanguard, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/10/tiktok-instagram-facebook-or-x-where-nigerians-are-building-audiences-and-what-each-platform-rewards/)). YouTube Shorts revenue sharing (45% creator share) applies to Nigerian creators ([BrandCom, Jan 2023](https://brandcom.ng/2023/01/11/youtubes-new-revenue-sharing-model-to-make-content-creators-earn-more/)). A Nigerian outlet reports a stricter Shorts threshold from February 2027 (UNVERIFIED against YouTube's own pages) ([BrandCom, 12 Aug 2026](https://brandcom.ng/2026/08/12/youtube-raises-bar-for-incoming-creators-introduces-tougher-monetization-rules/)). For most Nigerian creators, a $15 to $30 USD subscription is 15% or more of monthly creator income, and the platforms that pay Nigerians per view are Shorts and Facebook Reels, not TikTok.

Nigerians already work in the global clipping economy. Press describes clipping as "a gig anyone can pick up from a bedroom in Lagos or Lahore" ([CreatorDB, Aug 2026](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/)). Nigeria is not on Whop's sanctioned-countries list, but payout methods vary by country, non-US creators need a bank account in their registered country's currency, and Nigeria-specific payout behaviour could not be confirmed ([Whop payout methods](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-payouts/payout-methods); [Whop sanctioned countries, via snippet](https://docs.whop.com/trust-and-safety/trust-safety-overview/sanctioned-countries)). US-audience requirements and view-farm geofencing are likely to weigh against accounts with mostly Nigerian audiences, though no source says Nigeria is blocked.

Data and devices favour local, light processing. After the NCC approved tariff rises of up to 50% on 20 Jan 2025, the average data price was reported to rise from about ₦350 to about ₦525 per GB ([Legit.ng, Jan 2025](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/technology/1636594-new-cost-phone-call-data-mtn-airtel-9mobile-emerge-ncc-approves-tariff-hike/)). MTN's 75GB monthly plan went from ₦16,000 to ₦20,000 ([Naija News, 11 Feb 2025](https://www.naijanews.com/2025/02/11/mtn-increases-data-subscription-prices-see-new-data-bundle-prices/)). Median mobile download speed was about 44.1 Mbps in Q4 2025, ranked 85th globally ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/business-services/nigerias-internet-speed-slips-ranks-85th-globally/)). Android runs on about 88% of smartphones ([BusinessDay, Sep 2026](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/the-price-divide-behind-androids-88-dominance-in-nigeria/)), Transsion brands hold 65% of the market ([TechCabal, 5 Sep 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/05/nigeria-smartphone-market-recovery-q2-2025/)), and the average smartphone sold for $134 in Q1 2026 ([Flipbz](https://flipbz.org/smartphone-brands-in-nigeria-2026-flipbz-market-report-on-consumer-preferences-pricing-and-market-trends)). By rough arithmetic, a 1-hour 1080p upload of 1.5 to 4 GB to a cloud clipper costs about ₦800 to ₦2,100 at ₦525 per GB. Selects' first browser run downloads 0.9 to 2.2 GB of models once ([Selects README](../../README.md)). That is acceptable on Wi-Fi but heavy on mobile data, so the router's audio taggers must stay in the tens of megabytes. CapCut is the incumbent editor, with 383M global downloads in 2025 ([Udonis summary](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-apps/most-downloaded-mobile-apps)), and Nigerians publicly search for how to pay for CapCut Pro ([TikTok discover page, accessed 22 Oct 2026](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/how-to-pay-for-capcut-pro-in-nigeria)). Selects competes with "watch the whole video and cut it in CapCut" more than with OpusClip.

Language is the sharpest gap. Nigerian Pidgin has about 116M second-language speakers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Pidgin)), and 67% of Nigerians surveyed in 2021 said they watch comedy skits ([Africa Polling Institute, Jan 2021](https://africapolling.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/API-Skit-Comedy-Press-Release.pdf)). Whisper large-v3 scores 26.53% WER on the Nigeria subset of AfriSpeech-MultiBench, better than Parakeet ([arXiv 2511.14255, Nov 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14255)). Zero-shot Whisper on Yoruba exceeds 100% WER, and on code-switched speech Yoruba token error is near or above 97% for nearly every system tested ([NaijaVoices, 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20564); [arXiv 2609.11786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11786)). Among clippers, only Submagic documents Yoruba and Hausa captions and Kapwing Hausa transcription. OpusClip supports none of these languages, and nobody documents Pidgin ([Submagic help](https://care.submagic.co/en/article/new-52-languages-support-1qo3r75); [OpusClip FAQ](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/faqs)). Selects is already ahead on detection: its language check combines whisper, decode-and-compare, and two text models including lid-lite-5 for English, Hausa, Igbo, Pidgin and Yoruba. Its Nigerian-accent whisper cut unsure words on an elderly speaker from 20% to 2%, though no person has rated its accuracy yet ([Selects README](../../README.md)). The weakest point is that a correct language label does not produce a usable transcript for Yoruba or Hausa, and Igbo has no whisper model at all. That is why the router matters for Nigerian content in particular: laughter and crowd reaction carry skits and football-viewing-centre videos even where the words do not transcribe.

### Eval v3: one frozen clip world, pre-registered before any run

Eval v3 should reuse `eval/` (the dataset fetcher, `heatmap.mjs` labels, `metrics.mjs` AUC, Spearman, hit@k and IoU, sign-flip permutation tests, Holm correction) and add Come Through's frozen-world discipline ([PLAN.md, 17 Oct 2026](../../eval/PLAN.md); [Come Through FROZEN.md, 22 Oct 2026](../../../comethrough/eval/personas/FROZEN.md)). Five rules carry over. **Freeze the set:** a seeded builder writes `eval/v3/manifest.json`, `FROZEN.md` records its SHA-256, a `--check` mode rebuilds it, and the harness refuses to run on a mismatched hash. **Split dev and test:** stratify by content type, speaker region (African or other) and audio quality (from the transcript quality score). Dev is for finding problems. The test set runs once after the feature freeze, the harness writes a marker, and a second scored run is refused unless a reason is printed at the top of the results. **Record the world once:** yt-dlp info JSON, heatmaps, audio (and low-resolution video for visual items) are recorded on one date. This matters because Most Replayed curves change as views accumulate. Replay mode fails on any request that was not recorded, as `createWorld` does in Come Through ([world.mjs](../../../comethrough/eval/world.mjs)). **Cache the judge by prompt hash:** LLM answers are keyed by a SHA-256 of model, task, prompt hash and messages, so a changed prompt is a new key, and a ledger caps live Gemini calls. **No changes after test results** without a new freeze and a public note.

Sizing: eval v2's power estimate was about 52 videos for 80% power for Gemma 4 against chance at the observed effect ([RESULTS.md](../../eval/results/RESULTS.md)). So the talking-head test set should hold **60 videos (plus 30 dev)**. Each non-speech type should hold 15 test and 10 dev videos (comedy or reaction, sports, music or performance, gaming, vlog), pooled for the router's primary test and reported per type as secondary. CC BY videos with heatmaps may be scarce in some genres. The builder must report counts per stratum before freezing, and drop any stratum under 10 with a note. Cost stays zero: Gemma 4 through the free Gemini API with backoff and a ledger (v2 made 895 calls at 28 a minute for $0), local models elsewhere, and the v2 rule that the report states the n actually judged if quota runs out. Free-tier quotas are not published outside AI Studio ([Gemini rate limits, upd. 15 Oct 2026](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits)), so the run should spread across days.

Shared decision rule, fixed in advance: the five primary hypotheses (P1 to P5) are Holm-corrected at alpha 0.05, and the smallest effect of interest is **ΔAUC = 0.03**. A change ships as the default only if its Holm-adjusted p is below 0.05 and the lower bound of the bootstrap 95% CI is above 0. If the point estimate is at least 0.03 but not significant, it stays behind a flag and is reported as inconclusive. If the CI's upper bound is below 0.03, it is dropped.

### 1. Position-only baseline and honest labelling

**Evidence.** Position comes within 0.03 mAP of a fine-tuned transcript transformer on replay peaks ([Arora and Arora, NeurIPS 2026, abstract only](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/159082)). Vendors' own fine print calls their scores rankings ([OpusClip API blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-moment-detection-api)). Eval v2 lacked this control ([RESULTS.md](../../eval/results/RESULTS.md)). **Build.** Create a position prior from the dev set: the mean heatmap intensity by relative position, with the first and last 3% handled as `heatmap.mjs` already does. Show "rank in this video" in the UI with the measured AUC and interval, and keep the reason text. **Effort.** S. **Eval v3.** P1: shipped blend AUC minus position-only AUC is above 0 on the talking-head test set (windows R, unit = video). P2: blend plus position prior (weight fitted on dev only) minus blend is above 0. Baselines: random, energy, pre-score, position. Pass and fail follow the shared rule. If P1 fails, the README and UI must say the judge's lift over position is not measurable. If P2 passes, the position prior becomes part of the default ranker.

### 2. Audio-event path and router v0

**Evidence.** Crowd cheer dominates published sports fusion ([Merler et al., 2017](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.07075)), and audio alone placed 24 of 25 events within one second ([AWS/WBD, 2024](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/media/how-warner-bros-discovery-uses-audio-analysis-to-improve-data-accuracy-and-enrich-the-fan-experience)). Open laughter detectors reach reasonable event-level precision ([arXiv 2609.12642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12642)). An audio-capable model beat a vision-text model on engagement prediction ([Sun et al., 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02516v2)). **Build.** Stage 0 probes, rules-based Stage 1, and per-type proposals for comedy, sports and music. An unreliable transcript now switches path instead of stopping (the user is told why). Whisper non-speech tokens are free. YAMNet (browser) or EfficientAT mn10 (desktop) tag audio at 1 Hz. **Effort.** M. **Eval v3.** P4: on the pooled non-speech test set, the router's AUC against replay intensity is above 0.5. Secondary for P4: the share of non-speech test videos where Selects returns at least 3 candidates above its quality bar, router against current behaviour; and router type labels against Arnold's blind manual labels, made before the freeze, with macro-F1 of 0.7 or more needed to trust automatic routing (otherwise a user-chosen mode, as in OpusClip). P5: on talking-head plus comedy test videos, blend plus audio-event features (laughter and applause rates, loudness excursions over the speaker baseline) minus blend is above 0.

### 3. Learn from your results: per-creator calibration on the device

**Evidence.** The creator's history carries most predictable variance ([Martin et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01013); [MVP, 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00950)). Content matters more within one creator's catalogue ([Khosla et al., 2014](https://people.csail.mit.edu/khosla/papers/www2014_khosla.pdf)). Ten retrieved labelled examples added about 10 points to LLM prompting ([Kayal et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.597.pdf)). Retrieval-augmented models top the micro-video tables ([M3TR, 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15455v2)). One caution: Arora and Arora report channel-trained and genre-trained replay models transferring about equally (tables UNVERIFIED), so per-creator gains are not guaranteed. **Build.** Log every approve, skip, trim and render locally, plus optional posted outcomes the user enters or imports (views at 7 days, and average percentage viewed where the platform shows it). Fit a small logistic or isotonic model per creator over Gemma's four sub-scores, pre-score, position and audio-event features, and add the creator's own top past moments as few-shot examples for the judge. Everything stays in `~/.selects` or IndexedDB, nothing is uploaded, and there is an export and delete button. **Effort.** M. **Eval v3.** P3 runs in "creator worlds": creators with at least 8 CC BY videos with heatmaps (conference and lecture channels are likely). Videos are ordered by upload date; for each video t, the calibration fits on that creator's earlier videos (at least 3) and predicts t, with heatmaps as stand-in outcomes. Hypothesis: calibrated minus uncalibrated AUC is above 0, with the unit = creator. Target 12 or more creators. Below 12, P3 is reported as a pilot and leaves the Holm family, which says so in advance. Secondary: retrieval few-shot minus zero-shot, on the same windows.

### 4. Noisy-audio policy

**Evidence.** Two studies show denoising before Whisper raises WER ([arXiv 2512.17562](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.17562); [arXiv 2603.04710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04710)). Vendors enhance only the output ([Descript](https://descript.com/tools/voice-enhancer)). **Build.** Raw audio stays the default into whisper. DeepFilterNet runs on the rendered clip as an opt-in. Whisper trust feeds the router. **Effort.** S. **Eval v3.** S1 uses references that already exist: transcripts that Arnold or raters correct in Selects' own caption editor, which keeps word timings, plus creator-uploaded captions where present (availability UNVERIFIED). On a frozen noisy subset, compare raw, `--clean-audio`, DeepFilterNet-then-whisper, and `small.en`. Pre-registered expectations: DeepFilterNet-then-whisper WER is at least raw WER (a replication), and `--clean-audio` is non-inferior to raw within 1 WER point (bootstrap by speaker). If `--clean-audio` fails, Selects stops suggesting it. Also pre-register that the transcript quality score must correlate with WER at Spearman 0.5 or more before the router may rely on it.

### 5. African-language and accent support

**Evidence.** Whisper's 26.53% WER on Nigerian speech, the 97%+ Yoruba error on code-switched speech, and the market gap are covered above ([arXiv 2511.14255](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14255); [arXiv 2609.11786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11786)). Apache-licensed options exist for Pidgin (29.6% WER) and for Nigerian-accented English (Selects' shipped model). **Build.** Create a small frozen benchmark: about 20 minutes each of Nigerian English, Pidgin, Yoruba and Hausa (Igbo if a transcriber can be found), from CC BY sources or consenting speakers, transcribed by native speakers. Compare base.en, multilingual small, the Nigerian-accent model, pidgin-wav2vec2-xlsr53, Sunbird (after a licence check) and Gemma 4 E4B's 30-second ASR. Route per language only to models whose licence passes. **Effort.** M. **Eval v3.** S2: WER and CER per variety, bootstrap by speaker. A language's default switches only if WER improves by 5 points absolute or more, with a CI excluding 0 and a licence that passes. Secondary: on the African-speaker stratum of the v3 test set, compare router AUC with transcript-path AUC, to check whether non-speech signals hold up where words fail. NC datasets (NaijaVoices, AfriSpeech-200) may serve as extra evaluation references only, never for training a shipped model.

### 6. Retention-curve signals

**Evidence.** Exposure-normalised retention is the most content-predictable target, at Spearman about 0.70 ([Li et al., ECCV 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00289v1)). Early views predict later views well ([Szabo and Huberman](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0811.0405)). Most Replayed is public for videos with enough views, and tools like Headliner and OpusClip's MCP already read it ([Headliner, Apr 2025](https://soundsprofitable.com/press-release/headliner-launches-most-replayed-providing-a-proven-path-to-clip-creation/); [OpusClip MCP](https://www.opus.pro/mcp/inspiration/clip-the-exact-moments-your-viewers-rewatch-most)). **Build.** When a creator clips their own published video, Selects reads its heatmap through the existing fetcher as a prior. This fails closed, with no key, and is labelled as a signal that viewers already chose. Imported retention for posted clips feeds item 3. **Effort.** S to M. **Eval v3.** Using a video's own heatmap to rank that video is circular against a heatmap label, so it is not tested offline. Its value is measured only through P3's inputs and the S3 field pilot. That limitation is stated in advance, along with Mr. HiSum's self-fulfilling-bias warning.

### 7. Visual signals and local VLM captions

**Evidence.** Replay peaks cluster on visual moments ([Mr. HiSum, 2023](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/7f880e3a325b06e3601af1384a653038-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf)). Visual and aesthetic features drove most SnapUGC gains ([Li et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00289v1)). Selects' README admits the model "never sees the video". **Build.** ffmpeg scene cuts, frame-difference motion, face presence (macOS Vision today; MediaPipe elsewhere), and sparse OCR first. Then captions of 3 to 5 frames per shortlisted window from SmolVLM2 500M or Gemma 4 E2B where the device allows, added to Gemma's text dossier. **Effort.** L. **Eval v3.** S4: on the vlog, sports and gaming test strata, fusion with visual features minus fusion without them, AUC above 0; plus wall time per video on the 2017 laptop, with a pass threshold of no more than 1.5 times the current analysis time.

### 8. Router v1

**Build.** Replace the rules with per-type fusion weights fitted on dev, starting from IBM's 0.61 crowd weight for sports. **Effort.** L. **Eval v3.** Re-run P4 on a new freeze (v3.1), because weights fitted after seeing v3 test results would break rule 2.

### 9. A/B field test of picks and variants

**Evidence.** No academic study quantifies clip A/B gains on Shorts or TikTok, so this is a reasoned design, not cited evidence. Outcomes are noisy by nature ([Salganik et al., 2006](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/303s19/docs/salganik_dodds_watts.pdf)). Duplicate-looking posts risk platform and campaign penalties ([OpenClip guide](https://openclip.app/guides/whop-clipping-guide)). **Build.** A local posting log. For each long video the creator owns, Selects proposes its top pick, the position-baseline pick and a random pick of the same length: three distinct moments, so there are no duplicate posts. They are posted to YouTube Shorts or Facebook Reels (the platforms that pay Nigerians) in randomised order and time slots. Trim variants (hook-first or context-first) are compared across different moments, never by reposting one moment. **Effort.** M to build, months to yield. **Eval v3.** S3 is a pre-registered pilot of 10 videos (30 posts), reported descriptively: 7-day views and average percentage viewed, and the rank of Selects' pick among the three. The confirmatory sample size is computed from the pilot's observed variance and frozen before the confirmatory run. Nothing from the pilot is reported as a hypothesis test.

## Conclusion

Asking "why can others predict virality but Selects can't?" turns out to be a question about who publishes a number. Every vendor sells a label, and Selects is the only clipper found that has measured its label against an outcome and reported a confidence interval. AUC 0.56 at n = 19 is not a weakness to hide. It is the accurate figure for a problem the literature calls mostly unpredictable from content. The structural advantage that remains for well-funded vendors, pooled outcome data from connected accounts, is aimed at a variable that matters less than the creator's own history. A local tool can capture that history legally and free, one creator at a time.

On non-speech content, the industry's quiet lesson is that audio does most of the work and vision mostly confirms. That turns Selects' biggest gap into its cheapest fix: a 3.7M-parameter tagger and whisper's own confidence scores can route skits, matches, performances and noisy Lagos street audio away from a transcript that was never going to carry them. The order of work follows from the evidence. Measure against position first. Stop discarding videos the transcript cannot read. Then let each creator's own results, kept on their own device, become the training data no competitor can buy.
