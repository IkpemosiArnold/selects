# AI Clipping Market Map and Feature Matrix (as of 22 Oct 2026)

Method note: All vendor pricing pages were fetched directly with WebFetch on 22 Oct 2026 unless marked otherwise. Several pages render prices in JavaScript or in icons, so some cells could not be read; those are marked UNVERIFIED with the best dated secondary source. GitHub star counts were pulled from the GitHub REST API on 22 Oct 2026. "Checked" means 22 Oct 2026 for every row below unless stated.

## 1. Players and dated pricing (free tier, paid tiers, credits)

### Takeaway
The dedicated clippers cluster at USD 10 to 40 per month for an entry paid tier, priced on "credits" that almost always mean one minute of uploaded video. Free tiers exist for most (Opus, Vizard, Quso, Reap, Choppity, Spikes, 2short, Wisecut, Kapwing, Descript, Riverside) but are capped at 30 to 75 minutes a month, 720p or lower, short storage (3 to 7 days) and usually a watermark. Submagic and Munch have no free tier on their pricing pages (Munch is a 7-day trial).

### Inferences
- The effective market price of "AI clipping" is roughly $0.05 to $0.10 per input minute on entry plans (Opus Starter $15 / 150 min = $0.10; Quso Essential $39 / 300 = $0.13; Spikes PRO+ annual $14.09 / 300 = $0.05; Reap Creator annual $9.99 / 600 min = $0.017). Free tiers give 30 to 75 input minutes a month, which is one or two podcast episodes.
- Watermarks plus 720p plus 3 to 7 day storage are the standard free-tier squeeze. A zero-cost local tool with no watermark, 1080p, and no expiry beats every free tier on those three axes by default.
- Bundled players (Riverside, Descript, Canva, CapCut, Adobe Express) commoditise the basic feature; dedicated clippers now differentiate on API, scheduling, B-roll, and team features.

### Gaps
- Exact monthly (non-annual) prices for Klap, Vizard, Kapwing, and tier tables for VEED, CapCut, Canva, StreamLadder, Swiftia, Clipwise could not be rendered.
- No primary funding sources found for Vizard, Klap, Submagic, Munch, Quso, Reap, Spikes, Choppity, 2short, Swiftia. Opus funding figures conflict across aggregators.
- Riverside pricing came from a page labelled as a staging copy.

## 2. Feature matrix

### Takeaway
Face-tracked 9:16 reframe and animated captions are table stakes in every dedicated clipper. The paid differentiators are licensed or generated B-roll, dubbing/translation, auto-posting, public APIs, and a "virality score." African-language support is close to absent: only Submagic documents Yoruba and Hausa captions; OpusClip's 21-language list has no African languages; nobody documents Igbo or Nigerian Pidgin.

- Whisper large-v3 zero-shot Yoruba on FLEURS: CER 47.96 (reported on a fine-tuned model's card); a Yoruba dialect paper reports Whisper WER above 100 due to tonal diacritics. [BuzzASR/yoruba](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/yoruba); [arXiv 2406.19564](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.19564)
- A community Whisper large-v3 fine-tune on 51 African languages reports WER Swahili 0.087, Hausa 0.168, Yoruba 0.384 on small non-FLEURS dev subsets. [Sunbird SALT](https://salt.sunbird.ai/models/asr-whisper-51-african-languages/)

### Inferences
- "Accent handling" is not a documented claim on any vendor page fetched; vendors advertise language counts, not accent robustness. The only accent claim found is FunClip's Fun-ASR-Nano "26 regional accents," which refers to Chinese.
- Nigerian Pidgin is an open gap across the whole market, paid and free. Since Pidgin is English-lexified, Whisper English mode probably transcribes it partially, but there is no published benchmark here; Selects could own this by publishing a small Pidgin/Yoruba eval rather than claiming support.
- Only one vendor (Descript) publicly states a training opt-in posture; OpusClip and CapCut take perpetual broad licenses. "Nothing leaves your machine" is a credible, verifiable differentiator for a local tool.
- Non-speech/visual clipping is the weakest area for speech-first clippers; it exists mainly in gaming tools (Medal, Eklipse) and Canva's visual filtering.

### Gaps
- Per-tier icons on Vizard, Wisecut, Reap and others did not render, so B-roll, virality, reframe availability per tier is partly unknown.
- No vendor states data retention for paid tiers or whether user uploads train models, except Descript (opt-in) and partial OpusClip ToS language. Vizard, Klap, Submagic, Munch, Quso ToS were not fetched.
- Animated-caption and reframe confirmation for Klap, Munch, Quso, Choppity was not found on pricing pages (these are almost certainly present, but not verified here).
- No clean zero-shot Whisper WER for Hausa and Swahili on FLEURS was retrieved.

## 3. Where paid infrastructure creates the advantage, and the honest zero-cost alternative

### Takeaway
Most competitor advantages come from five paid inputs: licensed stock B-roll (Storyblocks), generative media (AI image/video B-roll, dubbing, lip-sync), hosted GPU/LLM inference, platform-approved posting integrations, and engagement data for "virality" scoring. A zero-cost local tool can match the core loop (transcribe, pick moments, reframe, caption) but cannot honestly match licensed B-roll libraries, voice dubbing quality, or data-trained virality prediction. Notably, the popular open-source clippers are not zero-cost either: they quietly depend on Gemini/OpenAI/MuAPI/ElevenLabs keys.

### Cited Findings
- Licensed stock: Submagic Pro/Business include Storyblocks B-roll and "Premium + Movie clips"; OpusClip Pro includes unlimited stock B-roll plus AI image and AI video B-roll. [Submagic](https://www.submagic.co/pricing); [Opus](https://www.opus.pro/pricing)
- Generative/dubbing: Reap meters dubbing separately (2 hr Creator, 5 hr Studio, 80+ languages); Kapwing meters dubbing (50 min Pro, 200 min Business); openshorts needs ElevenLabs for dubbing and fal.ai (Flux 2 Pro) for generated B-roll. [Reap](https://www.reap.video/pricing); [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/pricing); [openshorts](https://github.com/mutonby/openshorts)
- Hosted LLM dependency in OSS: openshorts requires a Gemini key "for all AI features"; AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator local mode "runs offline except for the LLM call" (OpenAI or Gemini), default mode needs a MuAPI key; FunClip's LLM clipping goes through keyed gateways. [openshorts](https://github.com/mutonby/openshorts); [AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator](https://github.com/Anil-matcha/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator); [FunClip](https://github.com/modelscope/FunClip)
- Posting integrations: openshorts uses Upload-Post (free tier 10 uploads/mo); vendors bundle posting into paid tiers (Opus Starter+, Quso Essential+, Vizard Creator+). [openshorts](https://github.com/mutonby/openshorts); [Opus](https://www.opus.pro/pricing); [Quso](https://quso.ai/pricing)
- Virality score is gated to paid tiers at Opus (not on Free). [Opus](https://www.opus.pro/pricing)
- Fully local components that exist today: faster-whisper transcription (CPU or CUDA), OpenCV face tracking, MediaPipe + YOLOv8 tracking, ffmpeg cropping; FunClip's Paraformer/SenseVoice and CAM++ diarization run locally under MIT/Apache. [AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator](https://github.com/Anil-matcha/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator); [openshorts](https://github.com/mutonby/openshorts); [FunClip](https://github.com/modelscope/FunClip)
- Whisper supports Yoruba, Hausa, Swahili tokens but not Igbo or Pidgin; zero-shot Yoruba is weak (CER ~48). [whisper tokenizer.py](https://github.com/openai/whisper/blob/main/whisper/tokenizer.py); [BuzzASR/yoruba](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/yoruba)

Honest zero-cost alternative per paid advantage (these are inferences unless cited above)
- GPU fleet for fast processing: local faster-whisper on CPU; slower (minutes per hour of audio on a laptop) but free. Honest framing: "slower, private, unlimited."
- Paid ASR with broad language lists: Whisper (99-language tokenizer set) locally; for Yoruba/Hausa/Swahili, swap in community fine-tunes (e.g., the Sunbird 51-language Whisper fine-tune) if their licenses allow. No zero-cost answer for Igbo or Pidgin today without fine-tuning.
- Hosted LLM moment picking: local Gemma (as Selects does) via llama.cpp/Ollama. Most OSS competitors do not do this; this is Selects's clearest open-source differentiator.
- Licensed stock B-roll: no honest zero-cost equal. Possible partial substitute is free-licence stock (e.g., Pexels/Pixabay APIs), but their API terms and key requirements were not verified here; otherwise skip B-roll and say so.
- AI dubbing / voice cloning: no honest zero-cost equal at quality; local TTS exists but was not evaluated. Recommend translated captions only.
- Virality score: competitors' scores presumably draw on engagement data they hold; a local LLM "hook strength" rubric is a heuristic, not a prediction, and should be labelled that way.
- Social auto-posting: requires platform API approvals or a third party (Upload-Post free tier 10/mo, which needs an account). Honest zero-cost path is "export and post manually," or open the platform's upload page.
- Non-speech clipping: local visual signals (scene change, laughter/applause audio events via SenseVoice-type models) are feasible but unproven here.

### Inferences
- The OSS field's star leaders (openshorts ~7k, FunClip ~6.4k, AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator ~5.5k) all either need a paid/keyed LLM or are Chinese-first. A clipper that is fully offline end to end, English plus African-language aware, with no keys at all, does not appear in the top results of GitHub search as of 22 Oct 2026.
- Since 2026 brought a wave of small "local Opus Clip alternative" repos (hotclip, Clips Kitty, chopify, cutawan, OpenClipper, clippyme), "local and free" alone is no longer unique; the defensible angles are zero keys, low-bandwidth/low-spec operation, and documented African-language quality.

### Gaps
- No source states how any vendor computes its virality score or what data it is trained on.
- No measured speed numbers for CPU-only local clipping were collected here.
- Free stock B-roll API terms (Pexels, Pixabay) were not fetched; whether they need a key/account, and whether that violates the "no account for users" constraint, is unverified.
- GitHub search may miss repos that do not use the words "shorts," "clips," or "Opus Clip alternative" in their descriptions.
