# The clipping economy and the African/Nigerian creator context for AI clipping

Research date: 22 Oct 2026. Context: Selects is an open-source, zero-cost clipper built by a Lagos-based developer.

Source-quality note: Many fetches of primary press (NPR, Forbes, TechCabal, Clipspeed) were blocked or returned 403. Where a figure comes from a secondary or vendor blog quoting a primary outlet, that is stated. Most clipper rates and earnings numbers are self-reported or vendor-reported and are marked UNVERIFIED. Several "sources" are blogs owned by clipping tools or marketplaces, which have a commercial interest in making clipping look lucrative.

## (A1) Whop Content Rewards: how it works, rates, budgets, payout rules, verification, fraud

### Takeaway
Whop Content Rewards is a self-serve marketplace where brands/creators post a campaign with a per-1,000-views rate and a budget, clippers post clips on their own TikTok/Reels/Shorts/X accounts and submit links, and Whop pays automatically for "verified" views after seller approval, taking a 10% fee. A September 2026 snapshot of 50 live campaigns put the median rate at $1.00 per 1,000 views (range $0.05 to $3.00), and Whop reportedly pays out around $40,000 a day, with bot fraud described by its own founder as the biggest threat.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanics (official docs): campaign setup, set payment rate and budget, creators post, the brand reviews submissions, then "after approving a submission, Whop automatically pays the content creator based on the number of views they got." Rate is set per 1,000 views. ([Whop docs, Content Rewards](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/growth-marketing/content-rewards), via search snippet; full page did not render on fetch)
- Snapshot study, 28 Sep 2026: author recorded all 50 campaigns in contentrewards.com/discover. Median $1.00/1K views. Distribution: under $0.50: 2; $0.50 to $0.99: 6; exactly $1.00: 19; $1.01 to $1.99: 11; $2.00 to $3.00: 12. Range $0.05 to $3.00. About $1.10M budgeted, about $784K spent across the 50. Examples: Yomi Denzel campaign spent $258,800 of $263,000; Jo Koy $8,700 of $10,000; Charlie Berens $4,600 of $6,400. ([Foundry, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/what-is-clipping-1-dollar-per-1000-views-50-campaigns))
- Same source: platforms are TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and X; clips are posted on the clipper's own accounts; Whop's "Content Reward Fee" is 10% of amounts paid to clippers; Whop counts only "legitimate views" excluding bots and paid inducements; sellers can reject only for breaking the brief/terms, fraud, or arriving after budget/end date. Example of per-clip minimum payout: $6 minimum at $3 rate means a clip needs about 2,000 views to enter review; example cap: $3,000 at $3 rate stops earning at 1M views. ([Foundry, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/what-is-clipping-1-dollar-per-1000-views-50-campaigns))
- Fee conflict: one guide says about 7% fee is "widely reported"; another source says Whop takes "about 9%". The 10% figure from Foundry is the most specific and recent. ([search summary citing clipaffiliates](https://www.clipaffiliates.com/blog/clipaffiliates-vs-whop); [Art of the Brand, 26 Sep 2026](https://artofthebrand.substack.com/p/how-clipping-became-a-billion-dollar))
- Third-party guides (UNVERIFIED): typical range $0.20 to $6 per 1K, average near $1; payment about 10 days after approval; clips earn for about 7 days. ([OpenClip guide](https://openclip.app/guides/whop-clipping-guide); [Clipspeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/whop-content-rewards-clipping-guide.html), via search snippet)
- Whop's own Content Rewards landing page showed example campaigns at $0.80 to $2.50 per 1K (page undated, appears old). ([Whop Content Rewards](https://whop.com/contentrewards/), via search snippet)
- OpusClip's blog (12 Aug 2026) on Content Rewards: music/logo campaigns about $1/1K; standard clipping $1 to $3; UGC/talking-head $2 to $50; typical live campaigns mid-2026 $0.50 to $1.50 with outliers up to $15 on small campaigns. Whop uses AI to flag suspicious submissions; review window about 72 hours. Flags concerns: view counts clustering near payout caps, unpaid submissions reported by some clippers, geo-restrictions bypassable with a VPN. ([OpusClip blog, Aug 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards)) Note: OpusClip markets itself as a tool for Content Rewards clippers; no formal integration described.
- One guide reports AI review auto-approves "legitimate" submissions within 48 hours (UNVERIFIED). ([search summary of third-party guides](https://www.clipaffiliates.com/blog/is-whop-content-rewards-legit))
- Scale: Forbes (April 2026, reported secondhand) said Content Rewards pays over $40,000 a day across nearly a million videos a month; clients reportedly include Polymarket, ElevenLabs and Justin Bieber. ([CreatorDB, Aug 2026](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/))
- Third-party tracker (Clippa, via Ascynd): about $2.58M paid to 8,466 earners across 6.6B views; about $887K paid in Feb 2026 alone (UNVERIFIED tracker estimate). That is roughly $305 per earner lifetime. ([Ascynd](https://ascynd.io/en/blog/whop-clipping); [Art of the Brand, Sep 2026](https://artofthebrand.substack.com/p/how-clipping-became-a-billion-dollar)) Note this conflicts in scale with the "$40K a day" figure; the tracker likely covers only a subset or earlier period.
- One guide claims about 98,000 registered clippers and over $1M/month brand spend (UNVERIFIED). ([NYSOS](https://nysos.io/blog/paid-clipping-platforms))
- Whop corporate: Tether invested US$200M at a US$1.6B valuation in Feb 2026; earlier Series B US$50M at US$800M (June 2024). Variety (26 Mar 2026) covered Content Rewards use in music marketing (Mumford & Sons, Brandi Carlile, John Summit). ([Wikipedia: Whop.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whop.com))
- Fraud: Content Rewards founder Daniel Bitton (18), quoted by Forbes, called bot fraud "the single biggest threat" and said clippers can buy fake views to inflate payouts. Verification approaches described in the industry: multi-layer bot detection, API-verified view counts pulled from TikTok/YouTube rather than screenshots, geofencing "known view-farm regions" (one platform named Pakistan, India, Indonesia), and buyer-side conversion tracking. ([TechBuzz, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/clipping-s-perfect-fraud-flywheel))
- A Stake (crypto casino) clip-farm network reportedly ran through Discord servers and a Whop marketplace; one account grew from 64K to 645K followers after switching to Stake content. ([TechBuzz, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/clipping-s-perfect-fraud-flywheel))
- Whop campaign geo rules exist at the campaign level: a Spencer Pratt LA mayoral campaign on Whop required posting to pages with at least 50% US-based audiences. ([Digiday, 18 May 2026](https://digiday.com/media/the-case-for-and-against-clipping/))

### Inferences
- The modal campaign pays $1 per 1,000 views, so 100K views earns about $100 gross, $90 after a 10% fee. Earning $1,000 needs roughly 1.1M verified views. Volume of output is the whole game, which is why clippers want speed tools.
- Per-clip minimum thresholds (example: about 2,000 views) mean many clips earn zero; clippers need to produce many candidates quickly, which favors automated moment-finding.
- "Audience geography" requirements (example: 50% US audience) plus geofencing of "view-farm regions" may disadvantage clippers whose accounts have mostly Nigerian/African audiences, even if Nigeria is not explicitly blocked.

### Gaps
- The official Whop docs page did not render; I could not confirm view verification method (OAuth account linking vs platform API vs scraping) from a primary Whop source.
- Content Rewards launch date not found.
- No official Whop data on clipper count or clipper geography.

## (A2) Clipping agencies, streamer/brand campaigns, press coverage, and how clippers find moments

### Takeaway
Clipping has become a mainstream, heavily covered marketing channel in 2025 to 2026 (Bloomberg Oct 2025, Forbes Feb/Apr 2026, Variety Mar 2026, NPR May 2026, Digiday May 2026, plus reported NY Mag/Verge/WIRED pieces), with rates of roughly $0.50 to $25 per 1,000 views, agency networks of 20,000 to 40,000 clippers, and growing controversy over undisclosed paid promotion, misleading clips, and fake views.

### Cited Findings
- Bloomberg (Oct 2025, via Digiday): Anthony Fujiwara's company Clipping earned about $7.7M in sales with over 20,000 contracted clippers in about 10 months. MrBeast (Jimmy Donaldson) previously paid Clipping $50 per 100,000 views ($0.50/1K). Forbes: a million views through Clipping can cost "a hundred dollars to a thousand dollars." ([Digiday, 18 May 2026](https://digiday.com/media/the-case-for-and-against-clipping/))
- NPR (12 May 2026, via Moneywise/Yahoo): rates from 50 cents to $25 per 1,000 views depending on content and sponsor (one agency offered $1/1K for baseball clips; an AI startup offered $25). Business Insider: $1 to $4 for some campaigns; "elite clippers" offered $500 to $1,500 monthly retainers plus performance pay. Emrah Bayraktar runs a network of about 40,000 freelance clippers. Clipping often done "with the help of AI tools" (no tools named). ([Moneywise via Yahoo Finance, 12 Jun 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/articles/clipping-side-hustle-youve-never-110000379.html); [NPR, 12 May 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/12/nx-s1-5794670-e1/the-clipping-economy-how-short-form-video-clippers-are-overrunningthe-internet))
- N3on (Kick streamer), via Business Insider reporting: reportedly paid $1.4M to 303 clippers over five weeks, about $2M to date; rate $40 per 100K views, raised to $50 at times; top clippers reportedly earn over $100K a month (UNVERIFIED); network of about 1,000 people, half recruited through a clipping group he built with Adin Ross across India and Nigeria. ([CreatorDB, Aug 2026](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/))
- Vyro (MrBeast-backed marketplace) pays $3 per 1K views and launched with over $100,000 paid to clippers for Beast Games. ([CreatorDB, Aug 2026](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/))
- CreatorDB census (30 days to 26 Aug 2026): 9,941 hashtagged clips of six streamers (IShowSpeed, Kai Cenat, N3on, CaseOh, Adin Ross, xQc) from 1,820 clipper accounts; 1.68B YouTube views and 260M TikTok plays (floors). Top fan-run channel "Kai Cenat Live" 16.3M subscribers. ([CreatorDB, Aug 2026](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/))
- Forbes (11 Feb 2026) "Inside the clipping farms driving fintech's marketing boom": $1 to $5 per thousand views; FTC had not yet acted against the clipping model specifically (via search summary; article fetch blocked). ([Forbes, Feb 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/boazsobrado/2026/02/11/inside-the-clipping-farms-driving-fintechs-marketing-boom/))
- Variety (26 Mar 2026): "What Is 'Clipping,' the Viral Marketing Strategy That's Taking Over the Music Biz?" covered Whop Content Rewards in music campaigns. ([Wikipedia citing Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whop.com)) Drake/Lil Baby/Ken Carson clipping claims found only in a low-authority blog; UNVERIFIED.
- Earnings distribution (UNVERIFIED, blog estimates): beginners $100 to $500/month, active $500 to $2,000, "elite" $20,000 to $30,000. Agency cuts range 20% to 80%. Typical $1 to $3/1K; finance/SaaS $5 to $6. ([Art of the Brand, 26 Sep 2026](https://artofthebrand.substack.com/p/how-clipping-became-a-billion-dollar))
- Reported press on fakery (secondhand via Art of the Brand): NY Magazine and The Verge (May 2026) reported agencies mobilizing thousands of accounts via Discord networks to fake organic virality; a WIRED investigation (April 2026) found an agency promoting a band by flooding TikTok with clips from accounts it created. ([Art of the Brand, Sep 2026](https://artofthebrand.substack.com/p/how-clipping-became-a-billion-dollar)) I could not fetch the originals.
- WSJ analysis (reported by CBS) found Polymarket's per-view clippers falsely depicted about $1.9M in winnings on TikTok; political clips for a Michigan Senate candidate paid $1 to $5/1K via Lumina Clippers, presented as organic (TRT World). ([search summary, Oct 2026](https://trtworld.com/article/3d3115552d5f))
- Moneywise/NPR controversy: streamer VitalyzdTv falsely accused Akash Singhania of being a predator; clips spread and his family was harassed. ([Moneywise via Yahoo, Jun 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/articles/clipping-side-hustle-youve-never-110000379.html))
- Platform policy pressure: Instagram's Mosseri (30 Apr 2026) said original creators should not "compete with accounts that simply re-upload"; undisclosed paid clips conflict with platform terms and FTC disclosure rules. ([Digiday, May 2026](https://digiday.com/media/the-case-for-and-against-clipping/)) Meta (Jul 2025) said accounts repeatedly reusing others' content can lose monetisation. ([Vanguard, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/10/tiktok-instagram-facebook-or-x-where-nigerians-are-building-audiences-and-what-each-platform-rewards/)) TikTok's Creator Rewards bans unoriginal/reposted content. ([Art of the Brand](https://artofthebrand.substack.com/p/how-clipping-became-a-billion-dollar))
- How clippers find moments: OpusClip markets an import-link, AI-ranks-by-virality, auto-caption, 9:16 reframe workflow for Content Rewards clippers and claims (anecdotal) 40+ clips from one long video in under 15 minutes. ([OpusClip, Aug 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards)) Multiple vendor blogs (Ssemble, Clippie, OpenClip, Clipspeed) publish "best AI tools for Whop clipping" guides, showing tool vendors target clippers. ([Ssemble](https://www.ssemble.com/fr/blog/best-ai-tools-for-whop-clipping-2026); [Clippie](https://clippie.ai/blog/how-to-make-money-with-whop-clipping))

### Inferences
- Clipper demand is a real market for a free tool: clippers are price-sensitive (median earnings are low, around $305 lifetime per Whop earner by one tracker), produce high volume, and are actively targeted by paid AI clippers (OpusClip, Ssemble, Clippie, etc.). A zero-cost tool removes a subscription that can exceed a beginner's monthly earnings.
- Risks for positioning: the economy is under reputational and policy pressure (fake views, undisclosed ads, reposting crackdowns). A tool marketed to clippers could be associated with "reupload spam." Positioning around creators clipping their own long-form content is safer.

- No data found on Andrew Tate's earlier clipping army, Clipping.app, ClipFarm or Clipr specifically.
- No survey data on what share of clippers use OpusClip vs manual CapCut editing.

## (A3) Nigerian participation in clipping and payout access

### Takeaway
Nigerians are explicitly part of the clipping workforce (N3on's network recruited in India and Nigeria; press describes clippers "in Lagos and Lahore"), and Nigeria is not on Whop's sanctioned-countries list, but payout friction and US-audience campaign rules are likely barriers; no Nigeria-specific clipper data exists.

### Cited Findings
- N3on's roughly 1,000-person clipping network: about half recruited through a group he built with Adin Ross across India and Nigeria. ([CreatorDB, Aug 2026](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/))
- Marketplaces turned clipping into "a gig anyone can pick up from a bedroom in Lagos or Lahore." ([CreatorDB, Aug 2026](https://creatordb.app/creator-news/the-rise-of-the-clip-economy/))
- Whop payouts: "over 200 countries"; methods (bank, mobile wallet, crypto, PayPal) vary by country; non-US creators need a bank account in their registered country's currency; no Nigeria-specific info in docs. ([Whop docs, payout methods](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-payouts/payout-methods))
- Nigeria is not on Whop's sanctioned-countries list. ([Whop docs, sanctioned countries](https://docs.whop.com/trust-and-safety/trust-safety-overview/sanctioned-countries), via search snippet)
- A clipping-platform feature board says payout-region issues are the "#1 reason" active clippers in certain regions drop off (user request, not Whop statement). ([ClippingBot board](https://clippingbot.userjot.com/board/p/region-matched-automated-payouts))
- Some campaigns require audiences at least 50% US-based. ([Digiday, May 2026](https://digiday.com/media/the-case-for-and-against-clipping/)) Some platforms geofence "known view-farm regions" (Pakistan, India, Indonesia named). ([TechBuzz, Jul 2026](https://www.techbuzz.ai/articles/clipping-s-perfect-fraud-flywheel))
- Nigerian streamer Peller held the highest-viewed stream in Africa (512K viewers), showing a domestic streaming scene that could generate clipping demand. ([Wikipedia: Peller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peller_(streamer)))

### Inferences
- Nigerian clippers are a plausible early user base for Selects: they already participate, face cost and payment frictions, and are least able to pay USD subscriptions for OpusClip-type tools.
- Nigerian clippers likely work mostly on US/global English content (where campaign budgets are), so English ASR quality matters more for this segment than Nigerian-language ASR.

### Gaps
- No data on number or share of Whop clippers in Nigeria/Africa.
- Could not confirm which Whop payout methods actually work for Nigerian accounts (naira bank, USD domiciliary, crypto).

## (B1) Tools used by Nigerian/African creators; CapCut dominance

### Takeaway
CapCut is the default mobile editor globally (about 383M downloads in 2025) and appears in Nigeria's Google Play top overall charts, but I found no rigorous Nigeria-specific survey of creator tool usage and no evidence of significant OpusClip adoption in Nigeria.

### Cited Findings

## (B2) Mobile data costs, devices, speeds, and implications for uploading long video

### Takeaway
Nigeria's data is cheap in USD terms (about $0.39/GB per Cable.co.uk-derived rankings) but rose sharply in naira after the NCC's January 2025 approval of up to 50% tariff hikes (average about ₦350 to ₦525 per GB), most phones are low-cost Android (Transsion 65% share, ASP about $134), and median mobile download is about 44 Mbps; uploading hour-long source videos to a cloud clipper is a real cost and friction.

### Cited Findings
- NCC approved a maximum 50% increase in calls, data and SMS on 20 Jan 2025 (operators asked for about 100%). Average data price reported to rise from about ₦350/GB to about ₦525/GB; a 1GB bundle from ₦1,000 to at least ₦1,400. ([Legit.ng, Jan 2025](https://www.legit.ng/business-economy/technology/1636594-new-cost-phone-call-data-mtn-airtel-9mobile-emerge-ncc-approves-tariff-hike/); [Tribune](https://tribuneonlineng.com/ncc-approval-how-new-50-tariff-hike-will-affect-calls-sms-data-rates/))
- MTN implementation (Feb 2025): 15GB weekly plan ₦2,000 to ₦6,000 (200%); 75GB monthly plan ₦16,000 to ₦20,000 (25%). ([The Condia](https://thecondia.com/mtn-nigeria-data-price-hike-2025/); [Naija News, 11 Feb 2025](https://www.naijanews.com/2025/02/11/mtn-increases-data-subscription-prices-see-new-data-bundle-prices/))
- Cable.co.uk-derived: Nigeria $0.39/GB, 31st cheapest globally, second cheapest in Africa; worldwide average about $2.60/GB. Original 2023 study: 5,603 plans in 237 countries, Nigeria range $0.13 to $1.64. ([Les Afriques, Oct 2026](https://lesafriques.com/en/2026/10/mobile-data-costs-across-africa-full-53-country-ranking-0-38-to-43-75-per-gb/); [Technext](https://technext24.com/news/nigeria-31-countries-cheapest-data-price/)) Caveat: the $0.39 figure appears to come from the 2023 dataset and likely predates the 2025 tariff hike; treat as stale.
- Ookla: Nigeria ranked 85th in mobile index (Dec 2025), down seven places over the year; median mobile download about 44.1 Mbps (Q4 2025). NCC reported average 4G download 33 Mbps by end-2025. Upload median not found. ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/business-services/nigerias-internet-speed-slips-ranks-85th-globally/); [tech.africa](https://tech.africa/ookla-speedtest-africa-2025/))
- Devices: Android on about 88% of respondents' smartphones (KPMG/Orange study, Sep 2026); smartphone ownership 75% in 2025 vs 64% in 2023. ([BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/the-price-divide-behind-androids-88-dominance-in-nigeria/)) Transsion (Tecno, Itel, Infinix) 65% market share per Canalys, strongest in sub-$100; feature phones 46% of total mobile market Q2 2025. ([TechCabal, 5 Sep 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/05/nigeria-smartphone-market-recovery-q2-2025/); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/technology/article/how-nigerias-top-smartphone-seller-transsion-became-worlds-4th-largest/)) Average smartphone selling price $134 in Q1 2026. ([Flipbz](https://flipbz.org/smartphone-brands-in-nigeria-2026-flipbz-market-report-on-consumer-preferences-pricing-and-market-trends))
- Internet users: 109M (45.5% penetration) in Oct 2025; Nigeria has about 130M people offline (third globally). Ad reach: TikTok 47.8M adults (+43.4% YoY), Facebook 38.0M, YouTube 30.5M, Instagram 10.0M, X 6.95M. ([DataReportal Digital 2026 via Vanguard, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/10/tiktok-instagram-facebook-or-x-where-nigerians-are-building-audiences-and-what-each-platform-rewards/); [DataReportal global overview](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-global-overview-report))

### Inferences
- Rough cost of a cloud-clipper upload (my calculation, not sourced): a 1-hour 1080p video is often 1.5 to 4 GB depending on bitrate. At about ₦525/GB that is roughly ₦800 to ₦2,100 per upload, before downloading rendered clips; on MTN's 75GB/₦20,000 plan (about ₦267/GB) roughly ₦400 to ₦1,100. For creators earning under $100/month (majority, see B4), repeated uploads are material.
- Clippers working on YouTube/Twitch content in a cloud tool avoid upload (link import), but a local tool must download the source; for a local-first free tool, the user pays the download instead. Supporting link import on a server, or processing low-res proxies/audio-only for moment detection, would cut data use. Audio-only transcription input is roughly 1/20th to 1/50th the size of video.
- Low-end Android dominance means on-device heavy ML (e.g., Whisper large) is unrealistic on phones; a desktop/laptop or lightweight server path is needed, or small models.

### Gaps
- No current (2026) official NCC or operator average price per GB found; no 2025/2026 Cable.co.uk primary page fetched.
- No data on typical RAM/storage of devices in use in Nigeria, or laptop ownership among creators.
- No Ookla median upload speed for Nigeria found (upload is what matters for cloud clippers).

## (B3) Languages: Pidgin, Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo in content, and ASR support

### Takeaway
Nigerian Pidgin is a national lingua franca (about 116M L2 speakers) and central to skit comedy, yet mainstream ASR coverage is weak: Whisper lists Yoruba and Hausa but not Igbo or Pidgin and performs poorly on Yoruba; OpusClip supports none of these. Specialist options now exist (Spitch, Intron Sahara, N-ATLaS, NaijaVoices dataset, community Whisper fine-tunes), but licensing and cost vary.

### Cited Findings
- Nigerian Pidgin: about 4.7M L1 and about 116M L2 speakers (2020). ([Wikipedia: Nigerian Pidgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Pidgin))
- 67% of Nigerians said they watch comedy skits (Africa Polling Institute, Jan 2021; interviews in English, Pidgin, Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba). ([API press release, 2021](https://africapolling.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/API-Skit-Comedy-Press-Release.pdf)) Academic study argues stand-up has given Pidgin prominence and prestige. ([UNILAG journal](https://lnr.unilag.edu.ng/article/download/898/727))
- Whisper's LANGUAGES list includes "yo": yoruba and "ha": hausa (also swahili, amharic, somali, lingala, shona) but not Igbo or Nigerian Pidgin. ([openai/whisper tokenizer.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/whisper/main/whisper/tokenizer.py))
- Whisper large-v3 zero-shot Yoruba WER on FLEURS about 1.014 (over 100%) per a fine-tune model card; fine-tuned 0.794. ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/Haramiday/owe-voice-whisper-large-v3-yoruba)) Code-switch study: Yoruba token error near or above 97% for nearly all systems tested including large-v3; large-v3 tends to drop tone marks/underdots. ([arXiv 2609.11786](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.11786)) Blog benchmark: large-v3 overall 35.05% WER on 500 Nigerian-accented samples (not peer-reviewed). ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/nadinev/whisper-keeps-correcting-nigerian-speech-heres-how-i-measured-it-4f4j))
- NaijaVox-V1 (community Whisper fine-tune) reports WER: Pidgin 16.8%, Nigerian English 21.1%, Yoruba 28.8%, Hausa 31.0%, Igbo 41.9%; licence CC-BY-NC 4.0 (non-commercial). ([HF: Axiveri/NaijaVox-V1](https://huggingface.co/Axiveri/NaijaVox-V1)) Self-reported, UNVERIFIED.
- NaijaVoices (Interspeech 2025): 1,800-hour speech-text dataset, 5,000+ speakers, Hausa/Igbo/Yoruba (no Pidgin). ([ISCA archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2025/emezue25_interspeech.pdf))
- Spitch offers STT/TTS for Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, English, Amharic (partnership announcement). ([iAfrica](https://iafrica.com/cencori-and-spitch-partner-to-give-african-developers-yoruba-hausa-igbo-and-amharic-voice-ai-through-one-api/)) Intron Sahara v2.5 advertises bilingual code-mixing for 12 African languages incl. Pidgin, Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba; vendor claims 73% error reduction, largest on Yoruba-English. ([Intron](https://www.intron.io/)) Intron's AfriVox-v2 benchmark: Nigerian Pidgin is the largest language (12,546 utterances). ([HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/intronhealth/afrivox-v2)) Both are paid commercial APIs (pricing not checked).
- N-ATLaS: released Sep 2025 by Nigerian government (NCAIR) and Awarri at UNGA80; Llama-3 8B fine-tuned on about 392M tokens; Yoruba, Hausa, Igbo, Nigerian-accented English; reportedly accompanied by language-specific ASR models; weights on Hugging Face under custom terms. ([TechCabal, 25 Sep 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/25/nigerian-government-awarri-launch-n-atlas/); [HF NCAIR1/N-ATLaS](https://huggingface.co/NCAIR1/N-ATLaS)) Third-party AfroBench eval: Yoruba 24.4 to 38.4, Igbo 27.7 to 38.7, Hausa 28.9 to 44.1 vs Llama-3-8B-Instruct. ([HF blog evaluation](https://huggingface.co/blog/seun-ajayi/n-atlas-evaluation-report)) One source dates the announcement Sep 2024, conflicting. ([TechSoma](https://techsoma.africa/nigeria-launches-n-atlas-v1-open-source-multilingual-ai-fluent-in-4-languages/))
- Meta MMS (2023) covers 1,100+ languages for ASR using Bible-reading data (about 32 hours per language). I could not confirm Igbo/Pidgin entries. ([Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/testing-the-massively-multilingual-speech-mms-model-that-supports-1162-languages-5db957ee1602))
- OpusClip: 21 languages, none African. ([OpusClip FAQ](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/faqs))

### Inferences
- For Selects, Nigerian-English and Pidgin content is a clear differentiation gap: no major clipping tool claims support. A zero-cost path could use Whisper (free, works passably on Nigerian-accented English) plus an optional free open-weight fine-tune for Pidgin/Yoruba/Hausa/Igbo, but licences (CC-BY-NC, N-ATLaS custom terms) must be checked; non-commercial licences are compatible with a free open-source tool only if the project itself is non-commercial and users accept those terms.
- Code-switching (English plus Pidgin plus Yoruba in one sentence) is the realistic case; transcript-based moment selection can still work if the English parts transcribe well, but captions in Pidgin will need user editing.

### Gaps

## (B4) Nigerian/African creator economy size and monetisation availability

### Takeaway
Nigeria's formally measured creator economy is small ($31.2M in 2025) and most creators earn under $100/month, while TikTok's Creator Rewards excludes Nigeria; YouTube (20B+ annual views of Nigerian content) and Meta (monetisation opened June 2024) are the main platform-pay routes, so brand deals and off-platform income dominate.

### Cited Findings
- Nigeria Creator Economy Report 2025 (Ministry of Art, Culture, Tourism and the Creative Economy; NCAC; TM Global; Communiqué): sector $31.2M in 2025, projected multibillion by 2030; Nigerian YouTube content over 20B annual views; over 1,500 channels past 100K subscribers; over $10M AdSense revenue in 2024; Instagram 45% of reported earnings; 56.45% of creators earn under $100/month, 3.23% above $5,000. Africa creator economy $3.08B (2023) projected $17.84B by 2030 (28.5% CAGR). ([TechCabal, 25 Sep 2025](https://techcabal.com/2025/09/25/at-31-2m-today-nigerias-creator-economy-could-be-worth-billions-by-2030/); [Communiqué](https://www.readcommunique.com/p/nigeria-maps-its-creator-economy)) Critics say $31.2M understates the sector. ([Business Cardinal](https://businesscardinal.com/creator-economy-nigeria-2025-market-growth-outlook/))
- Africa Creator Economy Report 2026 (TechCabal Insights and Communiqué) compares Nigeria, South Africa and Kenya; headline framing: "Infrastructure, not influence, defines creator earnings in Africa." ([TechCabal, 5 Feb 2026](https://techcabal.com/2026/02/05/africa-creator-economy-report/)) Full text blocked (403).
- TikTok Creator Rewards: Nigeria not among eligible countries (an Aug 2026 guide lists eight countries); Nigerian creators earn via LIVE gifts; one report says Scaled LIVE rewards available to select Nigerian creators. ([Vanguard, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/10/tiktok-instagram-facebook-or-x-where-nigerians-are-building-audiences-and-what-each-platform-rewards/); [YNaija](https://ynaija.com/will-tiktoks-new-payment-model-finally-benefit-nigerian-creators-or-are-we-still-locked-out/))
- Meta opened monetisation to Nigerian creators in June 2024 (in-stream ads, Reels ads, bonuses, subscriptions). X replaced Creator Revenue Sharing with "Original Content Rewards" on 8 Sep 2026; Nigerian eligibility unconfirmed. ([Vanguard, Oct 2026](https://www.vanguardngr.com/2026/10/tiktok-instagram-facebook-or-x-where-nigerians-are-building-audiences-and-what-each-platform-rewards/))
- YouTube Shorts revenue sharing (from 1 Feb 2023) applies to Nigerian creators, 45% creator share. A Nigerian outlet reports a new rule requiring 10M qualified Shorts views per rolling 90 days to keep Shorts revenue (effective Feb 2027) (UNVERIFIED, not confirmed against YouTube's own pages). ([BrandCom, 12 Aug 2026](https://brandcom.ng/2026/08/12/youtube-raises-bar-for-incoming-creators-introduces-tougher-monetization-rules/); [BrandCom, Jan 2023](https://brandcom.ng/2023/01/11/youtubes-new-revenue-sharing-model-to-make-content-creators-earn-more/))
- Nigeria moved to tax the creator economy (Selar row, Aug 2026). ([TechCabal, 14 Aug 2026](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/14/nigeria-creator-economy-tax/))

### Inferences
- With most Nigerian creators earning under $100/month and USD-priced AI clippers costing roughly $15 to $30/month (other researchers should confirm prices), a paid clipper is out of reach for the majority; a zero-cost tool fits the market.
- Because TikTok does not pay Nigerians per view, Nigerian creators' clip volume is driven by audience growth and brand deals, and YouTube Shorts plus Facebook Reels are the platforms that actually pay. Selects' output presets should prioritize Shorts and Reels alongside TikTok.

### Gaps
- No primary YouTube data on YPP availability in Nigeria (widely assumed available; not confirmed in this pass).
- Africa Creator Economy Report 2026 details not accessible.
