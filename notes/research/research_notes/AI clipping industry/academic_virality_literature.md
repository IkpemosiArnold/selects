# Academic literature: short-video popularity prediction and within-video engagement (highlight / Most Replayed) prediction

Research date: 22 Oct 2026. Every number below was read from the primary paper text (PDF or arXiv HTML) unless marked UNVERIFIED. Metric shorthand: SRC/SROCC = Spearman rank correlation; PLCC = Pearson; nMSE = MSE divided by label variance (1.0 means "no better than predicting the mean", so R^2 is roughly 1 minus nMSE); MAP = mean average precision; P@K = precision at K.

Context to interpret: Selects (whisper transcript plus Gemma LLM judge) ranked segments against YouTube Most Replayed at AUC 0.56 (95% CI 0.49 to 0.62, n = 19 videos).

## Q1. What features drive short-video popularity, and what accuracy do content-only vs social/meta features get?

### Takeaway
Across a decade of papers, content-only models predict cross-video popularity (views/likes) weakly: Spearman about 0.3 to 0.55 and R^2 usually under 0.1 to 0.3, while creator features (follower count, the creator's past success) carry most of the predictable signal. The exception is exposure-normalized engagement (watch percentage, "did viewers keep watching past 5 s"), where content-only models reach Spearman about 0.70, because normalizing by views strips out the reach effect that creators and the algorithm control.

### Cited Findings

Image and post popularity (the foundational ablations)
- Khosla, Das Sarma and Hamid, "What makes an image popular?", WWW 2014 (about 2.3M Flickr images, Spearman on log-normalized views). Content-only (combined gist, color, texture, gradient, deep, object features) gets Spearman 0.31 (one-per-user set), 0.36 (user-mix), 0.40 (user-specific); social-only gets 0.77 / 0.66 / 0.21; content plus social gets 0.81 / 0.72 / 0.48. A single feature, the user's mean views across all their photos, alone reaches 0.75 in the one-per-user setting. ([Khosla et al. 2014 PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/khosla/papers/www2014_khosla.pdf))
- Same paper: in the user-specific setting (a separate model per creator, ranking that creator's own images), content-only (0.40) beats social-only (0.21), i.e. content signal is strongest when the creator is held fixed. ([Khosla et al. 2014 PDF](https://people.csail.mit.edu/khosla/papers/www2014_khosla.pdf))
- SMP Challenge overview (Wu et al., ACM MM 2023; SMPD dataset of about 500K Flickr posts from about 70K users; metrics SRC and MAE). Baselines: image-only ResNeXt-101 + LightGBM SRC 0.322; BERT text features 0.538; GPT-2 text 0.574; FastText 0.622; FastText + ResNeXt 0.70; 2020 winners 0.674 and 0.704; 2022 winner 0.71 (MAE 1.24). The overview says the top-1 team's SRC rose from 0.59 to 0.77 over the challenge years. Note that SMPD posts carry user/tag metadata and the text features include user-written tags, so these are not clean content-only numbers. ([SMP Challenge overview, arXiv 2405.10497](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.10497))

Micro-video popularity (Vine, Xigua, TikTok, MicroLens)
- Chen et al. TMALL (ACM MM 2016) and successors on the NUS Vine micro-video dataset (303,242 Vine videos, 6 to 8 s; popularity is the average of comments, reposts, likes, loops; four modalities: visual, acoustic, text, social). Reported nMSE (lower is better, 1.0 = mean predictor): ELM 0.982, TMALL 0.979, TLRMVR 0.934, MMVED-REG 0.914. That is roughly 2% to 9% of variance explained even with a social modality included. ([Xie et al., "Predicting the Popularity of Micro-videos with Multimodal Variational Encoder-Decoder Framework", arXiv 2003.12724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.12724))
- Same MMVED paper, Xigua dataset (3.2M samples, predicting a temporal popularity sequence): MMVED-TMP nMSE-TMP 0.971, SRC 0.767 (SRC here measures trend agreement of the popularity time series, not cross-video ranking). Dropping social, text, visual or audio at test time barely moves results (SRC 0.761 to 0.767). ([arXiv 2003.12724](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.12724))
- M3TR (arXiv 2411.15455, Shanghai Jiao Tong; retrieval-augmented, temporal) results table on MicroLens-100k: M3TR nMSE 0.705, SRC 0.559, PLCC 0.557; best baseline MMRA nMSE 0.785, SRC 0.495; TMALL SRC 0.440; MMVED SRC 0.353; SVR SRC 0.405. On the 2024 INFORMS TikTok challenge data: M3TR nMSE 0.854, SRC 0.433; MMRA SRC 0.387; CBAN SRC 0.221. Modality ablation on MicroLens: removing audio drops SRC 0.559 to 0.525, removing visual to 0.519, removing text to 0.534. ([M3TR, arXiv 2411.15455](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15455v2))
- SMP Challenge 2025 Video Track winner MVP (Ye et al., ACM MM 2025; SMPD-Video: 6,000 TikTok-style video posts from 4,500 users; label log2(views/days + 1); metric MAPE). Full model MAPE 0.1754. Ablations: without user profile features MAPE jumps to 0.3010 (the largest effect by far); without video embedding 0.1818; without text embedding 0.1810; without tag popularity 0.1785; without metadata 0.1782; without posting time 0.1758 (posting time nearly irrelevant). Top CatBoost features: LikeCount 13.8, VideoCount 12.4, HeartCount 12.0, FollowerCount 10.0 (creator-level stats), then SuggestedWordsLen 4.2, AvgTagPopularity 3.8; video-embedding dimensions sit near 0.8 to 0.9. ([MVP, arXiv 2507.00950](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00950))
- Paekivi and Karjus (arXiv 2606.16053, June 2026, preprint): 9,654 TikTok videos from 30 Estonian brand/organization accounts, 77 content variables annotated by Gemini 2.5 Pro. Content variables add only R^2 0.056 to 0.078 beyond follower count plus video age; full-model R^2 is 0.267 (likes), 0.183 (comments), 0.162 (shares), 0.266 (saves), with split-to-split SD up to about 0.15. Useful features were mid-level "packaging" (calls to action, original spoken audio, tone, setting, direct address); narrative archetypes did not predict. ([EmergentMind summary of arXiv 2606.16053](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.16053))

Exposure-normalized engagement (content-only, much more predictable)
- Li et al., "Delving Deep into Engagement Prediction of Short Videos", ECCV 2024 (SnapUGC: about 90,000 Snapchat Spotlight videos, 10 to 60 s, more than 2,000 views each). Labels deliberately avoid views and likes because "recommendation systems strongly influence" view counts and big-follower creators get more views; they use normalized average watch percentage (NAWP) and engagement continuation rate (ECR, share of viewers who watch more than 5 s). Content-only model: NAWP SRCC 0.696 / PLCC 0.701; ECR SRCC 0.675 / PLCC 0.688. Video-quality models alone: DOVER NAWP SRCC 0.635. Incremental ablation (NAWP SRCC): VQA visual 0.625, plus audio 0.636, plus title/description 0.651, plus captions 0.657, plus mPLUG-2 visual 0.689, plus aesthetics 0.696. Transcripts gave no improvement (only about 30% of videos had useful speech). Classic quality MOS correlates with watch time only 0.07 to 0.31. ([Li et al. ECCV 2024, arXiv 2410.00289](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00289v1))

Information cascades (the cleanest "how much does content explain" ablation)
- Martin, Hofman, Sharma, Anderson and Watts, "Exploring limits to prediction in complex social systems", WWW 2016 (Twitter URL cascades). Content-only features (basic plus topic): "negligibly low R^2", below 0.05 even with the content's past success; basic user features alone R^2 close to 0.2; adding the user's past success: R^2 0.42 (unrestricted) to 0.48 (restricted). A single feature, past user success, "performs almost as well" as all features combined. Simulations suggest even perfect knowledge would leave bounds well below deterministic accuracy. ([Martin et al. 2016 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01013))
- Martin et al. summarize Bakshy et al. (WSDM 2011): the most informative feature was the seed user's past success; content added nothing after that; best R^2 about 0.34 while calibration on averages was R^2 about 0.98, meaning individual outcomes are noisy even when averages are predictable. ([Martin et al. 2016 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01013))

### Inferences
- A content-only cross-video popularity predictor should be expected to land around Spearman 0.3 to 0.5 and R^2 0.05 to 0.3 on raw views/likes. Creator history roughly doubles the explained variance in every ablation found (Khosla, Martin, MVP).
- The features that matter in content are mid-level: audio presence/type, text/hook packaging, aesthetics, calls to action. Deep "story" constructs and posting time contribute little.
- Exposure-normalized targets (retention, watch percentage) are the realistic content-only target: about 0.70 Spearman is achievable there, versus about 0.3 to 0.5 for raw counts.

### Gaps
- Pinto, Almeida and Goncalves (WSDM 2013) early-view-pattern numbers were not retrieved: UNVERIFIED.
- MicroLens dataset paper (Ni et al., 2023) details and the original MMRA venue (believed SIGIR 2024) were not verified; MMRA numbers above come only from the M3TR comparison table. The exact SIGIR 2024 title search returned nothing: UNVERIFIED.
- Whether the MicroLens/INFORMS setups in M3TR include creator features was not confirmed.
- The official SMP 2025 leaderboard was not found; MVP's first-place claim is self-reported.

## Q2. Evidence that early performance, the recommendation algorithm and randomness dominate outcomes

### Takeaway
Early view counts predict final popularity far better than any content model (log-scale Pearson 0.92 to 0.98), and controlled experiments show that social feedback loops make success partly unpredictable even when the content is held identical. On algorithmic feeds like TikTok, reach is decoupled from follower count, which increases per-video variance.

### Cited Findings
- Salganik, Dodds and Watts, "Experimental Study of Inequality and Unpredictability in an Artificial Cultural Market", Science 311 (2006). 14,341 participants, 48 unknown songs, 8 independent "social influence worlds" plus an independent condition. All eight social-influence worlds were more unequal than the independent world; stronger social signals increased inequality further; the same song's market share varied more across social worlds than across independent subpopulations, "inherent to the process and cannot be eliminated simply by knowing more about the songs". "The best songs rarely did poorly, and the worst rarely did well, but any other result was possible." The authors expect real-world social influence to be far stronger than in the experiment. ([Salganik et al. 2006 PDF](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/303s19/docs/salganik_dodds_watts.pdf))
- Martin et al. WWW 2016: best ex-ante model explains less than half the variance (R^2 0.48) of Twitter cascades; simulations show that even small uncertainty in content "quality" sharply lowers the achievable bound; bounds for platforms with less data "are likely even lower". ([Martin et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01013))
- Szabo and Huberman, "Predicting the popularity of online content" (arXiv 0811.0405, 2008; published in Communications of the ACM 2010, venue UNVERIFIED in the fetched text). Log-transformed early vs 30-day popularity: Digg Pearson 0.98 after the 5th hour and 0.993 after 12 hours; YouTube 0.92 after about 5 days (only 0.7 untransformed); day-7 vs day-30 YouTube R = 0.77. Digg needs about 2 hours of data for a 30-day forecast, YouTube about 10 days. ([Szabo and Huberman, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0811.0405))
- Cheng, Adamic, Dow, Kleinberg and Leskovec, "Can cascades be predicted?", WWW 2014 (Facebook photo reshares): relative growth "becomes more predictable as we observe more of its reshares"; temporal and structural features of the early cascade are key predictors. Exact accuracy (a Cornell student blog cites 72.5% for predicting whether a cascade doubles) is UNVERIFIED. ([Cheng et al. abstract, arXiv 1403.4608](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.4608))
- Guinaudeau, Munger and Votta, Computational Communication Research 2022 (11,546 political TikTok accounts, nearly 2M videos, compared with top 33 political YouTube channels): an account's most popular TikTok has 64 times as many views as its average video, versus 40 times on YouTube; the For You Page "replaced following behavior", and virality happens "regardless of an account's number of followers". ([Penn State / TechXplore write-up, DOI 10.5117/CCR2022.2.004.GUIN](https://techxplore.com/news/2022-12-tiktok-lowers-barriers-virality-tight.pdf))
- Li et al. ECCV 2024 (Snap) note engagement labels are "biased by the recommendation system's preference distribution and by sampling bias from limited initial viewers", but do not quantify a noise ceiling. ([arXiv 2410.00289](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00289v1))

### Inferences
- For a clipping tool, the honest framing is "raise the odds" rather than "predict virality": the same clip can land very differently depending on the first test batch, and the literature gives no evidence that content-only models escape this.
- The highest-value extra signal is early performance (first hours/days), followed by the creator's own history. Both are available to a creator after posting, not before.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that directly measures how much TikTok/Shorts/Reels views are explained by follower count vs content under the For You algorithm. Vendor claims (for example Quso.ai's 508K-post analysis) were not used because methodology is undocumented.
- Exact Gini and unpredictability values from Salganik et al. are in figures and were not transcribed.

## Q3. Within-video engagement: how well do content models predict YouTube Most Replayed, retention, and highlights?

### Takeaway
Predicting Most Replayed from content is hard and gains over chance are small. Visual-only models beat random by roughly 5 to 7 points of precision, crowd humans perform at chance, and a 2026 transcript-based study finds that a simple position-in-video baseline comes within 0.03 mAP of a fine-tuned transformer. Selects' AUC 0.56 is therefore in line with the literature, not a sign of a broken pipeline.

### Cited Findings

Most Replayed as a label
- Sul, Han and Lee, "Mr. HiSum", NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks. 31,892 YouTube-8M videos (121 to 300 s, mean 201.9 s), each with at least 50,000 views; label is YouTube's Most Replayed curve (100 normalized scores per video); music videos excluded because replay depends on audio. Inputs are visual only (Inception-v3 1 fps features). Test set of 2,000 videos. Benchmarks (F1 / MAP at rho = 50% / MAP at rho = 15%): PGL-SUM 55.89 / 61.60 / 27.45; VASNet 55.26 / 58.69 / 25.28; SL-module 55.31 / 58.63 / 24.95; DSNet 50.78 / 57.31 / 24.35; iPTNet 50.53 / 55.53 / 22.74. ([Mr. HiSum paper PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/7f880e3a325b06e3601af1384a653038-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf))
- Mr. HiSum user study: 25 videos, 30 annotators; human majority vote agreed with Most Replayed labels on 64.8% of clips, disagreed on 19.6%, tie 15.6%. Authors flag a "self-fulfilling bias": once the heatmap appears in the UI (after 50,000 views), viewers may replay parts because the UI marks them. Training on Mr. HiSum improves transfer to TVSum (PGL-SUM F1 55.5 to 57.1) and SumMe (41.7 to 42.3). ([Mr. HiSum PDF](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/7f880e3a325b06e3601af1384a653038-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf))
- Duico, Strafforello and van Gemert (TU Delft/TNO), "Can we predict the Most Replayed data of video streaming platforms?" (arXiv 2309.06102, 2023; venue not stated). YTMR500: 500 Creative Commons vlog/travel videos, 3 to 20 min, at least 30k views, visual-only I3D features. P@K on 100 segments (random = 15 / 30 / 50): PGL-SUM 22.0 / 37.9 / 56.8; fully-connected 21.5 / 37.6 / 56.3. On 10-segment user-study videos (random 10 / 30 / 50 for P@1/3/5): MTurk humans 9.6 / 31.8 / 51.3 (chance level, Krippendorff alpha -0.017); PGL-SUM 17.5 / 39.2 / 58.5. Authors: models are "only marginally better than random" and users are "not able to perform significantly better than random"; they suggest the label is noisy and video-only input insufficient. ([Duico et al., ar5iv 2309.06102](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.06102))
- Arora and Arora, "Text Barely Beats Timing: Predicting YouTube Replay Peaks from Transcripts", listed at NeurIPS 2026 (abstract only seen, via search snippet; tables UNVERIFIED). 13,008 videos across five genre corpora, five single-channel corpora and five held-out channels; fine-tuned RoBERTa with title cross-attention vs chance, position, TF-IDF and BiLSTM. Position-only scores come within 0.03 mAP of the transformer on most corpora; the transformer adds +0.022 to +0.027 mAP on genre corpora, most where position is weakest; on held-out channels models beat position on 3 of 5 channels; conclusion: most predictable structure in replay behavior is temporal, with text adding a small signal. ([NeurIPS 2026 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/159082))
- Sahu and Pandey, "A global predicted-fMRI drive signal from TRIBE does not predict YouTube replay heatmaps" (arXiv 2607.01400, 2026 preprint). On 48 videos, a brain-encoding model's predicted engagement curve had position-controlled partial r +0.058 (95% CI -0.04 to 0.15, p = 0.23); loudness baseline +0.04; motion -0.06; video-level Spearman vs views -0.09 (not significant). ([Hugging Face paper page 2607.01400](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.01400))

Highlight detection / summarization benchmarks with LLMs
- LLMVS, "Video Summarization with Large Language Models" (arXiv 2504.11199, CVPR 2025 per figure filenames, venue UNVERIFIED): LLaVA-1.5 captions per frame, Llama-2-13B scores importance. Kendall tau / Spearman rho: Human 0.177 / 0.204 on TVSum and 0.205 / 0.213 on SumMe; random 0 / 0. A zero-shot LLM over captions scored only 0.051 / 0.056 on TVSum (0.170 / 0.189 on SumMe); the trained LLMVS reached 0.211 / 0.275 (TVSum) and 0.253 / 0.282 (SumMe). Zero-shot transfer to 50 Mr. HiSum videos: LLMVS tau 0.440 vs PGL-SUM 0.375, VASNet 0.364. ([LLMVS, arXiv 2504.11199](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.11199))
- QVHighlights (query-based highlight detection): third-party leaderboard tops are SG-DETR with pretraining Hit@1 71.00, mAP 44.70; VideoLights-B-pt 70.56 / 42.84 (aggregator numbers, not re-checked against papers). An MLLM frame-segmentation approach (arXiv 2512.12246, Dec 2025) reports HIT@1 56.74 using 25 frames. ([HyperAI QVHighlights board](https://vercel.hyper.ai/en/sota/tasks/highlight-detection/benchmark/highlight-detection-on-qvhighlights); [arXiv 2512.12246](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12246v1))
- SVHighlights (arXiv 2606.06926, 2026): LLM saliency over captions plus transcripts for long sports video; adding transcripts to captions improves mAP over captions alone; Llama3-8B beats Llama2-7B (exact numbers UNVERIFIED). ([arXiv 2606.06926](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.06926))

Retention curves
- No paper was found that predicts full audience retention curves for online video from content. The closest are SnapUGC NAWP/ECR (video-level watch percentage, content-only SRCC about 0.70) and the Most Replayed work above. ([Li et al. ECCV 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.00289v1))

### Inferences
- Translating to Selects' metric: AUC 0.56 means a randomly chosen Most Replayed segment outranks a non-replayed one 56% of the time (Somers' D about 0.12). Duico et al.'s P@15 of 22% vs 15% chance and Mr. HiSum's MAP at 15% of about 27 (chance roughly 15, my estimate) are the same order of weak-but-real lift, achieved with supervised training on thousands of videos. A zero-shot LLM on text alone, with no training on replay labels, landing at 0.56 is unsurprising; the LLMVS zero-shot caption baseline on TVSum (rho 0.056) was even closer to chance.
- With n = 19 the CI (0.49 to 0.62) cannot separate Selects from chance or from the about 0.6 range the supervised literature implies. Roughly 100+ videos would be needed for a tight estimate; Mr. HiSum argues 1,000+ videos for stable evaluation of these tasks.
- The most important control Selects is missing is a position-only baseline (score by time in video: intros, endings and "skip-to" points carry replay peaks). Arora and Arora find position nearly matches a fine-tuned transformer on transcripts, and the TRIBE study reports position-controlled correlations. If Selects' 0.56 is mostly position, the LLM may add nothing; if position alone scores near 0.5, the LLM is contributing.
- Most Replayed measures re-watching, not "best standalone clip". It also excludes music videos in Mr. HiSum, rewards visual moments (goals, reveals) a transcript cannot see, and carries a UI feedback bias. A transcript-only judge has a structural ceiling against this label.

### Gaps
- Full results tables of "Text Barely Beats Timing" (NeurIPS 2026) could not be fetched (page 404); only abstract numbers are reported. No AUC numbers on Most Replayed were found in any paper, so direct metric comparison requires conversion.
- No paper was found that evaluates a general MLLM (Gemini, GPT-4o, Qwen-VL) zero-shot against Most Replayed heatmaps.
- The Mr.HiSum random-baseline MAP was not reported in the paper; the "about 50 and about 15" chance levels are my inference.

## Q4. 2024 to 2026 work using multimodal LLMs / VLMs for short-video engagement, and honest results

### Takeaway
Fine-tuned VLMs are now state of the art on content-only engagement benchmarks, but the gain is modest (SnapUGC SRCC 0.69 to 0.71 vs 0.70 for a 2024 non-LLM model). Zero-shot LLM prompting is near chance on hard targets and only becomes strong with retrieval of labeled neighbors and supervised signals.

### Cited Findings
- Sun et al., "Engagement Prediction of Short Videos with Large Multimodal Models" (arXiv 2508.02516, Aug 2025; 1st place ICCV 2025 VQualA EVQA-SnapUGC challenge). SnapUGC splits 106,192 train / 6,000 val / 8,459 test; label ECR. Test results (SROCC / PLCC / final score = 0.6 SROCC + 0.4 PLCC): MinimalisticVQA 0.587 / 0.582 / 0.585; Li24 (ECCV 2024 model) 0.657 / 0.665 / 0.660; fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL 0.665 / 0.662 / 0.664; fine-tuned VideoLLaMA2 (frames, text, audio) 0.691 / 0.701 / 0.695. Four-model ensemble in the challenge: 0.707 / 0.714 / 0.710, next team 0.698. Using 60% of training data still gives 0.691. No creator or follower metadata used. ([Sun et al., arXiv 2508.02516](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.02516v2))
- Kayal et al., "Large Language Models Are Natural Video Popularity Predictors", ACL 2025 Findings. Task is binary and balanced on extreme classes of already-trending YouTube videos: 6,279 "local hits" (bottom 25% in views and countries trended) vs 7,360 "global big hits" (top 25% in both). Models: Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Llama 3 (Gemini 1.5 Pro robustness check). Vanilla LLM prompt: 59.0% (title only) and 62.5% (title plus VLM frame descriptions). Adding reasoning and few-shot: 62.5% / 66.0%; adding 10 nearest labeled examples: 71.5% / 72.0%; supervised content-embedding baseline 80%; LLM with hypothesis generation 82%; LLM plus supervised signal 85.5%. ([Kayal et al. ACL Findings 2025 PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.597.pdf))
- Paekivi and Karjus 2026 used Gemini 2.5 Pro as an annotator of 77 theory-driven variables; content variables added only R^2 0.056 to 0.078 over followers plus age on brand TikTok videos, and human validation showed deeper semiotic constructs were unreliable (Krippendorff alpha 0.07 and -0.09). ([arXiv 2606.16053 summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2606.16053))
- A 2026 social-media popularity benchmark (arXiv 2606.27539) used Gemma-3-12b-it as a zero-shot baseline on Bluesky cascades and reports much higher error than a specialized model (exact numbers UNVERIFIED). ([arXiv 2606.27539](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27539))

### Inferences
- Zero-shot LLM judging, which is what Selects does, sits at the weak end of every result found (59 to 62.5% on an easy balanced extreme-class task; rho about 0.05 for zero-shot caption scoring on TVSum). The big gains in these papers come from (a) fine-tuning on engagement labels and (b) retrieving labeled nearest neighbors as context. Both are possible for Selects with a modest labeled set.
- Kayal's 85.5% should not be read as "LLMs predict virality": the task drops the hard middle 50% and the "global reach" label correlates with language and region.

### Gaps
- No 2024 to 2026 paper was found that reports zero-shot Gemini/GPT-4o accuracy on raw short-video view prediction across the full distribution.
- The VQualA 2025 challenge report (Li et al., ICCV 2025 Workshops) was not fetched for the full leaderboard.

## Q5. Synthesis: realistic ceiling for a content-only tool, and which extra signals help

### Takeaway
A realistic ceiling for a content-only tool is about Spearman 0.3 to 0.5 on cross-video views/likes, about 0.70 on exposure-normalized retention, and only slightly above chance (AUC roughly 0.55 to 0.65 equivalent, inferred) for within-video Most Replayed ranking. The proven boosters are the creator's own history (largest effect in every ablation), early post-launch performance, per-creator models, and retrieval of labeled similar examples.

### Cited Findings
- Creator history dominates: Martin et al. content-only R^2 below 0.05 vs past user success 0.42 to 0.48 ([Martin et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.01013)); Khosla user mean views alone Spearman 0.75 vs content 0.31 ([Khosla 2014](https://people.csail.mit.edu/khosla/papers/www2014_khosla.pdf)); MVP removing user profile raises MAPE from 0.175 to 0.301, while removing video embeddings only to 0.182 ([MVP 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00950)).
- Per-creator modeling makes content matter more: Khosla's user-specific content-only Spearman 0.40 exceeds social-only 0.21 within a creator's own catalog ([Khosla 2014](https://people.csail.mit.edu/khosla/papers/www2014_khosla.pdf)). Arora and Arora find channel-trained and genre-trained replay models transfer equally to held-out channels (abstract-level, tables UNVERIFIED) ([NeurIPS 2026 listing](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/159082)).
- Early performance: log-scale correlation 0.92 at 5 days (YouTube) and 0.98 after 5 hours (Digg) with 30-day popularity ([Szabo and Huberman](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0811.0405)).
- Retrieval of labeled similar videos: MMRA and M3TR (retrieval-augmented) top the MicroLens table (SRC 0.495 and 0.559 vs 0.35 to 0.47 for non-retrieval baselines); M3TR without retrieval drops to SRC 0.505 ([M3TR](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15455v2)); Kayal's 10-nearest-example prompting adds about 10 points over vanilla prompting ([Kayal 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.597.pdf)).
- Timing/position priors: for within-video replay, position nearly equals text models ([Arora and Arora, NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/159082)); posting time contributes almost nothing to cross-video popularity in MVP (MAPE 0.1754 to 0.1758) ([MVP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00950)).
- Irreducible randomness: social influence makes identical content diverge across worlds ([Salganik et al. 2006](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/303s19/docs/salganik_dodds_watts.pdf)); TikTok's best video per account gets 64 times average views ([Guinaudeau et al. 2022 via TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2022-12-tiktok-lowers-barriers-virality-tight.pdf)).

### Inferences
- Recommended positioning for Selects: sell it as "find candidate moments fast and rank them a bit better than chance", not as virality prediction. A 0.56 AUC from zero-shot transcript judging is about what the literature predicts; claims of 80 to 90% virality accuracy from vendors are not supported by any content-only academic result found.
- Highest-leverage upgrades, in evidence order: (1) add a position-only baseline and report LLM lift over it; (2) fuse position priors with the LLM score; (3) add per-creator calibration from a creator's own past clips and their performance; (4) use retrieved labeled examples (few-shot with real Most Replayed peaks from the same genre/channel); (5) add non-transcript signals (audio energy, visual change, on-screen text), since replay peaks are often visual and SnapUGC found visual and audio features drove most gains; (6) close the loop with post-launch data (early views) and posting multiple clip variants as a cheap A/B test.
- Evaluate on 100+ videos, and also on the target that matters for the product (clip watch-through or retention), which the literature shows is more content-predictable than Most Replayed.

### Gaps
- No academic study was found that quantifies thumbnail/title or clip A/B testing gains on YouTube or TikTok; the A/B recommendation is reasoning, not cited evidence.
- No paper reports a formal noise ceiling (for example split-half reliability of Most Replayed curves or SnapUGC labels), so the "ceiling" numbers are empirical bests, not theoretical limits.
