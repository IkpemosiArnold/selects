# Send speech, not models, from Lagos phones

*Research report for Selects Web, written 22 Oct 2026. Synthesised from six research notes in `notes/research/research_notes/Slow internet first clip/`, the builders' verified findings of 22 Oct 2026, and a read of the repo (no source code changed). All live tests named below ran from a Mac in Lagos on an Airtel line on 22 Oct 2026; they are desktop measurements, not phone measurements, unless a row says otherwise.*

The fastest and most reliable route to a first reviewable clip on Nigerian mobile data is to never make the phone download a speech model at all: decode the audio on the device, cut it at pauses into exact-offset pieces, compress each piece to Opus at 16 kbps (0.12 MB per minute, builder-measured), send the pieces in order to Groq's free whisper-large-v3 (Cloudflare Workers AI free tier as failover), rank candidates as soon as the first few minutes of words return, and show a playable preview with captions drawn in the page before any 9:16 file is rendered. That is "Light mode", which the builders shipped in its first form on 22 Oct 2026; what remains is to make it progressive and to fix the two download paths that broke on Arnold's iPhone. On a peak-hour budget 4G line (1 Mbps up), a 30-minute talk then costs about 5 MB in total and should reach a first preview in roughly 1 to 2 minutes, against 6 or more minutes just to fetch today's 140 MB whisper set over a connection that drops it and starts again from zero. Light mode is also the more accurate option, not a compromise: hosted large-v3 sits near 26.5% word error on Nigerian speakers, while the base-sized models a phone can carry have no published Nigerian score at all, and Selects' own test found base.en unsure of 20% of words on an elderly Nigerian speaker. Private mode (nothing leaves the device) stays worth keeping, but it needs Range-resumable downloads, a smaller q8 whisper set, a zero-download preliminary ranking so the 0.7 to 2 GB judge is not on the critical path, and honest expectations of 10 or more minutes to a first clip on a mid-range phone. Every option that needs a card on file, including Cloudflare R2, is out under the zero-cost gate, and the only accounts Arnold needs are a free Groq account and a free Cloudflare account.

## Lagos uploads run at 2 to 8 Mbps and collapse within minutes

Headline speed tests flatter Nigeria. Ookla's user-started tests put the national mobile median at **43.4 Mbps down, 11.1 Mbps up and 34 ms latency in September 2026**, flat for a year and under half the global median ([Speedtest Global Index, Nigeria, Sep 2026](https://www.speedtest.net/global-index/nigeria)). Background-measured experience is far lower: Opensignal's April to June 2026 data gives **MTN 21.3 / 7.9 Mbps, Airtel 14.2 / 4.3, Glo 13.0 / 5.0 and T2 17.6 / 1.9** (down / up), with Airtel meeting "consistent quality" thresholds in only **6.9%** of tests ([Opensignal Nigeria, Jul 2026](https://insights.opensignal.com/reports/2026/07/nigeria/mobile-network-experience)); nPerf's 2025 barometer agrees (MTN 18.7 / 8.7, Airtel 10.6 / 4.7) ([ITEdgeNews, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.itedgenews.africa/mtn-tops-nigerias-mobile-internet-rankings-in-2025-as-competition-intensifies-nperf-report/)). Upload, not download, is the binding constraint for any design that sends anything up. No public source gives 10th or 25th percentile speeds for Nigeria, so the bad-case profile below rests on the weakest operator medians plus observed behaviour.

That behaviour is the second problem. In a live test on an Airtel router in Lagos (22 Oct 2026, 14:17 to 14:29 UTC, desktop), download throughput fell from **19.75 Mbps to 3.78 Mbps within about 10 minutes**, one TLS handshake took 2.6 s, Cloudflare served the same client from Lagos for one hostname and Paris for another, and traceroute showed carrier-grade NAT (research note `nigeria_network_devices.md`). The NCC logged **413,587 consumer complaints in H1 2026**, Airtel alone 228,992 ([Techeconomy, 29 Sep 2026](https://techeconomy.ng/ncc-nigeria-telecom-complaints-hit-413587-in-h1-as-data-billing-issues-persist)), and reported 19,384 fibre cuts in 2025 plus peak-hour congestion ([Techeconomy, 28 May 2026](https://www.techeconomy.ng/nigeria-telecom-data-calculators-data-depletion-complaints)). 4G carries **54.3%** of mobile connections, 5G only **4.49%**, and 2G/3G still over 41% (NCC data for May 2026, via [Techeconomy, 31 Aug 2026](https://techeconomy.ng/5g-lags-as-4g-commands-543-of-nigerias-mobile-connections)); about 55% of Lagos had no 5G coverage at the end of 2025 ([AllAfrica, 31 Aug 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202608310321.html)). A 140 MB single-stream download over 6 to 20 minutes on such a link has a real chance of being cut, which is consistent with what happened on Arnold's iPhone.

Data is cheap per gigabyte only on large bundles. At the Feb 2026 list prices, the small bundles people actually buy cost **NGN 350 to 750 per GB** (MTN or Airtel 1.5 GB weekly at NGN 1,000 is NGN 667/GB, about USD 0.50), big monthly bundles fall to NGN 120 to 200 per GB, and out-of-bundle pay-as-you-go costs **NGN 3,070 (MTN) to NGN 4,600 (Airtel) per GB** ([Mobility.com.ng, 27 Feb 2026](https://mobility.com.ng/best-data-plans-in-nigeria-2026-updated-prices/); USD at NGN 1,333 per [Nairametrics, 13 Oct 2026](https://nairametrics.com/2026/10/13/naira-holds-near-n1333-as-fx-turnover-swings/)). Most monthly plans add 2 to 4 GB of "night" data valid from midnight to 5 AM, and bonus or night data never rolls over (same source). On a 1.5 GB weekly bundle, Private mode's 0.9 to 2.2 GB first run is the user's entire week.

The phones are modest. In September 2026 Nigerian mobile web traffic was **84% Android, 16% iOS**; Tecno, Infinix and itel together were about 42%, Samsung 17%, Apple 16%, Xiaomi 13% ([StatCounter OS](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/nigeria) and [vendor](https://gs.statcounter.com/vendor-market-share/mobile/nigeria), Sep 2026). The volume models carry 3 to 4 GB of RAM on Unisoc T7250 or MediaTek Helio G81 chips (for example the Tecno Pop 10 with 3 GB, per [Mobility.com.ng](https://mobility.com.ng/tecno-pop-10-specs-price/), and the Spark 40C with 4 or 8 GB, per [GSMArena](https://m.gsmarena.com/tecno_spark_40c-14052.php)). iPhones are mostly UK-used 11 to 13 models at NGN 120,000 to 300,000 ([Revibyte, 25 Apr 2026](https://www.revibyte.blog/posts/best-iphones-under-300k-nigeria-2026/)), with 3 to 4 GB of RAM ([9to5Mac, Nov 2023](https://9to5mac.com/2023/11/18/iphone-ram-list/)). Browsers split **Chrome 68%, Opera 18%, Safari 11%**, with Samsung Internet and Transsion's Phoenix at about 1% or less ([StatCounter mobile browsers, Sep 2026](https://gs.statcounter.com/browser-market-share/mobile/nigeria)). Opera's share includes some Opera Mini, which cannot run this app.

## Every on-device model costs tens of megabytes and minutes of phone CPU

Today Selects Web loads `onnx-community/whisper-base.en_timestamped` through transformers.js 4.3.1: an fp32 encoder (82.5 MB) plus a q8 decoder (53.7 MB) for WebAssembly, or the same encoder plus a q4 decoder (123.7 MB) for WebGPU. With tokenizer files that is the **140 MB and 209 MB** that failed on Arnold's phone (Hugging Face tree listings, [whisper-base.en_timestamped](https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/whisper-base.en_timestamped/tree/main/onnx), queried 22 Oct 2026). Private mode then adds a judge: Gemma 3 1B (722 MB) without WebGPU or Gemma 4 E2B (2,004 MB) with it (repo `notes/MODELS.md`), which is where the **0.9 to 2.2 GB** before a first clip comes from.

The cheapest saving needs no new model. In these timestamped exports the "q4" decoder is larger than the q8 one (base 123.7 MB against 53.7 MB, probably because the q4 export leaves the 106 MB token-embedding table unquantised), so an all-q8 set is the small option: **whisper base q8 is 76.9 MB and tiny.en q8 is 40.8 MB**, against 136 MB of weights today (research note `on_device_stt.md`). Whether the q8 encoder costs accuracy on Nigerian speech is not established anywhere and must be measured on Selects' own clips. The multilingual base is the same size as base.en, and on AfriSpeech the multilingual Whisper models beat their .en twins at both small (0.391 against 0.414 WER) and medium sizes ([AfriSpeech-200, arXiv 2310.00274, Sep 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00274v1)), so multilingual base q8 deserves a place in the test.

Every speed figure in the sources is desktop or server; **no public benchmark gives a real-time factor or peak memory for any of these models in a phone browser.** On a 2017 dual-core laptop, Selects' own browser whisper base.en ran at **0.75x to 1.9x real time on WebAssembly and 0.31x on WebGPU** (repo `notes/timings.md`, desktop, heavy load), so a 30-minute video is likely to take 15 to 40 minutes or more to transcribe on a Helio phone.

**Reconciling Moonshine.** The on-device note found that Moonshine's own runtime (`@moonshine-ai/moonshine-wasm`) gained cross-attention word timestamps in March 2026 ([docs/word-level-timestamps.md](https://github.com/moonshine-ai/moonshine/blob/main/docs/word-level-timestamps.md)), while the builders verified that the transformers.js path returns no word timestamps and that the ONNX exports expose no cross-attention outputs for DTW. Both are true of different runtimes, and the builder finding governs Selects: adopting Moonshine would mean a second runtime whose default build needs `COEP: require-corp` for threads, with word timing quality nobody has measured. Treat Moonshine as unsuitable until that runtime is tested.

**The browser's Web Speech API is not an answer.** On-device recognition shipped only in desktop Chrome 139+, English only as en-US, with about 60 MB per language pack; on Chrome for Android `available()` returns "unavailable" ([chromestatus 6090916291674112](https://chromestatus.com/feature/6090916291674112); [field test, 11 May 2026](https://bagrounds.org/ai-blog/2026-05-11-1-word-meter-android-rca)). Feeding a file's audio through `start(track)` works only in desktop Chrome 135+ and runs at playback speed; no Android browser or Safari supports it ([MDN browser-compat-data, Oct 2026](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/api/SpeechRecognition.json)). There are no word timestamps anywhere in the API; segment times are due in desktop Chrome 159 on 8 Dec 2026 ([chromestatus 5811907077472256](https://chromestatus.com/feature/5811907077472256)). Without on-device mode the audio goes to Google anyway.

### WebKit sets the ceiling on iPhones

On iPhone every browser is WebKit, and four WebKit facts shape the design. First, WebKit still does not implement `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: credentialless`: [WebKit bug 230550](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=230550) is status NEW, last touched 7 Sep 2026. Selects sends COOP `same-origin` with COEP `credentialless` (confirmed live on selects.ikpemosi.work, 22 Oct 2026), so on iOS `crossOriginIsolated` is false and the ONNX Runtime bundle Selects ships forces `numThreads = 1` (read in `onnxruntime-web` 1.31.0-dev.20260914 in the repo). Whisper on an iPhone therefore runs single-threaded today. Serving `COEP: require-corp` to WebKit (chosen when `window.credentialless` is absent) would restore threads; Hugging Face answers model fetches with `access-control-allow-origin`, but every other cross-origin subresource would then need CORP headers. Second, WASM SIMD is available from iOS 16.4 and WebGPU from iOS 26 ([caniuse WebGPU](https://caniuse.com/webgpu), read 22 Oct 2026), but ONNX Runtime's JSEP/WebGPU and asyncify builds have a reported memory runaway on Safari 26 (1 GB to 14 GB, closed by the stale bot on 18 Jul 2026 without a fix), while the plain WASM build stayed stable ([onnxruntime #26827](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/issues/26827)). Selects' built bundle includes a 26.9 MB `ort-wasm-simd-threaded.asyncify` file (4.6 MB brotli, measured on `dist-web` on 22 Oct 2026), so the WebKit path should be checked against this bug. Third, a WebContent process is killed at a soft limit of about **1.5 GB** on most iPhones, about 2 GB on newer ones, and the page simply reloads ([WebKit bug 277848](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=277848); [Apple forums 823061](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/823061)); older transformers.js Whisper crash reports on iOS remain unresolved ([transformers.js #1242](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js/issues/1242)). Fourth, Safari deletes all script-writable storage after seven days of Safari use without a visit, so a downloaded model can vanish ([WebKit blog, 24 Mar 2020](https://webkit.org/blog/10218/full-third-party-cookie-blocking-and-more/)); `navigator.storage.persist()` and Home Screen install reduce that risk ([WebKit storage policy, 10 Aug 2023](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)).

On Chrome for Android, COEP credentialless has worked since Chrome 96, so multithreaded WebAssembly is available, and WebGPU is on by default for Android 12+ on Qualcomm and ARM GPUs since Chrome 121 ([Chrome for Developers, Jan 2024](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-121)). Reports through September 2026 describe WebGPU on Mali and PowerVR as buggy or blocklisted in practice ([playcanvas #8874](https://github.com/playcanvas/engine/issues/8874)), and Selects' WebGPU set is the larger one anyway, so multithreaded WASM with a q8 model is the dependable Android path.

**Reconciling the Groq evidence.** The hosted-transcription note could not confirm whether the published limits applied to the free plan (the table is labelled "base limits for the Developer plan") or whether signup needed a card, and it favoured whisper-large-v3-turbo on price. The builders have since verified that the free tier gives the same 2,000 requests and 28,800 audio-seconds a day, needs no card, returns 429 at the limit and is reachable over Airtel from Lagos; those facts win. The proxy defaults to `whisper-large-v3`, which is the right call on a free tier: both models draw on the same quota, and Groq's own table gives v3 **10.3% WER against 12% for turbo**. Turbo stays a configuration switch.

The quota is the real limit. 28,800 audio-seconds is **8 hours of speech a day per Groq organisation**, so the shared proxy key covers about 16 thirty-minute jobs a day, and Groq's 20 requests a minute will produce 429s once several people upload at once. The Cloudflare failover adds about 214 minutes (about 7 more such jobs) and a far higher 720 requests a minute. The "bring your own free Groq key" option already in the app gives each heavy user their own 8 hours. Self-hosting is not a better free path: free GPU routes (a Hugging Face ZeroGPU Space, Modal's monthly credit) exist but add cold starts and undocumented quotas, and Modal's card requirement was not checked, so they are backups to investigate, not plans.

Accuracy favours the hosted route. Off-the-shelf Whisper large-v3 scores **26.53% WER on Nigerian speakers** in AfriSpeech-MultiBench, against 32.60% for Parakeet 0.6B and 12.83% for Intron's commercial Sahara V2 ([arXiv 2511.14255](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.14255v1)); no study reports tiny or base. The cheap mitigation on the hosted path is Groq's `prompt` (or Cloudflare's `initial_prompt`) seeded with names and Pidgin terms from the video title.

## A 30-minute talk is 3.6 MB of Opus, and pieces make it resumable

The audio path decides how many bytes cross the uplink. The builders measured **0.42 MB for a 3.5-minute clip at Opus 16 kbps**, which matches the arithmetic of 0.12 MB per minute.

| Format the phone sends | MB per minute | 30-minute talk | Upload time at 1 Mbps (P1) | Upload time at 5 Mbps (P2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Opus 16 kbps (Ogg) | 0.12 | 3.6 MB | 29 s | 6 s |
| Opus or AAC 24 kbps | 0.18 | 5.4 MB | 43 s | 9 s |
| MP3 32 kbps (WASM encoder) | 0.24 | 7.2 MB | 58 s | 12 s |
| WAV, 16 kHz 16-bit mono | 1.92 | 58 MB | 7.7 min | 1.5 min |
| The video's own AAC track at 128 kbps | 0.96 | 29 MB | 3.8 min | 46 s |
| The video itself (repo test file: 1 hour, 114 MB) | about 1.9 | about 57 MB | 7.6 min | 1.5 min |

Encoder support is broad where it matters. In a crowd dataset collected through 2026, WebCodecs Opus encoding works in **100% of Chrome Android sessions and 78.1% of Safari iOS sessions** ([webcodecsfundamentals.org Opus page](https://webcodecsfundamentals.org/codecs/opus.html), read 22 Oct 2026; decoder data since Jan 2026, encoder dates not given). The missing fifth on iOS matches the browsers older than Safari 26, which added `AudioEncoder` and `AudioDecoder` to WebCodecs in September 2025 ([WebKit, Safari 26.0](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)); AAC shows the same 78% on iOS ([mp4a.40.2 page](https://webcodecsfundamentals.org/codecs/mp4a.40.2.html)). Opera Mobile is listed as supporting WebCodecs ([caniuse WebCodecs](https://caniuse.com/webcodecs)) but is not broken out per codec, so Opera for Android needs a device test. Selects' `encode.js` already tries Opus at 16 kbps first, then AAC at 24 kbps, then MP3 at 32 kbps through mediabunny's WASM encoder, then WAV in short pieces; if a smaller WASM Opus fallback is wanted, `opus-recorder` wraps libopus 1.3.1 in WebAssembly ([npm, opus-recorder](https://npmjs.com/package/opus-recorder)).

The bigger iOS gap is decoding, not encoding. Before Safari 26 there is no WebCodecs `AudioDecoder`, so mediabunny's `canDecode()` is false and Selects stops with "This browser cannot decode the video's audio" in every mode. iPhone XR and XS cannot install iOS 26 or 27 ([AppleInsider, 8 Jun 2026](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/06/08/ios-27-keeps-iphone-11-and-newer-compatibility)). The fallback is to copy the AAC packets without decoding them, in 5 to 10 minute pieces, and hand each to Web Audio `decodeAudioData`, which old Safari has (a design proposal from the video note, not yet tested).

**Silence gating must keep exact time.** Silero VAD is MIT-licensed, about 2 MB, and processes a 30 ms chunk in under 1 ms on one CPU thread ([snakers4/silero-vad](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad), read 22 Oct 2026); a browser wrapper on ONNX Runtime Web exists (ricky0123/vad, linked from the same page). But Selects already learned what naive VAD costs: whisper.cpp's built-in Silero VAD moved word start times by a **median of 0.2 to 0.7 s and up to 2.6 to 4.0 s**, and squashed 1.9 s pauses to 1.0 s (repo `notes/MODELS.md`). Captions, cut snapping and pause-based sentences all depend on those times. The rule that follows is the one `speech-chunks.js` already implements: each piece is one contiguous stretch of source audio with a known start offset, never audio glued across a gap, cut at the quietest 50 ms near the limit, with regions padded by 0.3 s and only silences of 2 s or more dropped. A Silero detector can replace the loudness rule to drop more dead air, provided its output feeds the same offset map and keeps padding around every region.

Resumable upload comes free with this shape. The first piece is 30 s (about 60 kB) and later pieces are up to 2 minutes (about 0.24 MB), each retried on its own, so a dropped connection costs one piece, not the job. A protocol such as tus or multipart upload solves the problem of single large files; it is unnecessary when no request is bigger than a quarter of a megabyte. Groq's 25 MB free-tier file cap and Cloudflare's 100 MB request cap are irrelevant at these sizes, and the proxy base64-encodes for Cloudflare on the server, so the phone never pays the 33% base64 overhead.

## Order of operations, not bandwidth, decides time to first clip

Reading the engine shows the main remaining delay: in both modes the candidate stage runs only after the whole transcript exists (`src/browser/engine.js`), and in Private mode the model download starts only after the whole audio has been decoded. The fix is to make the pipeline progressive, which the research supports. Long-form ASR already works in overlapping 30 s windows ([Hugging Face, ASR chunking](https://huggingface.co/blog/asr-chunking)), and streaming systems confirm text only when consecutive passes agree ([whisper_streaming, arXiv 2307.14743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14743)). For Selects the sequence is: decode the first 10-minute audio segment and start sending pieces while the rest decodes; score candidate windows with the existing model-free first stage (sentence shape, pauses, hooks, loudness, laughter) as soon as they lie wholly inside returned text; show the top one to three as "preliminary" with a preview that seeks the source video and draws captions in the page; send the shortlist text to the judge in small batches (about 5 kB for 8 windows of 100 words); re-rank as later pieces arrive, pinning any clip the user is viewing. A reviewable clip does not need a rendered 9:16 file, and rendering only after approval removes encode time from the critical path. How much ranking quality is lost by judging the first N minutes is unmeasured; Selects' eval set can measure it as rank agreement between prefix and full-transcript top three.

**Downloads must resume.** The brief named `src/browser/cache.js` as the likely root cause, and the code confirms it does one plain `fetch()` piped into `cache.put()`, with no Range request, retry or resume. The repo shows one correction to that diagnosis: whisper is not fetched by `cache.js`. It is fetched by transformers.js's own loader (`env.useBrowserCache`, Cache Storage `transformers-cache`), which also reads each file whole and stores it after the full body arrives (only a `bytes=0-0` probe uses Range, per `utils/hub.js` and `get_file_metadata.js` in transformers.js 4.3.1). `cache.js` is the loader for the 0.7 to 2 GB judge. Both paths share the flaw, and a drop at 95% restarts either from zero. Hugging Face's byte host supports the fix today: a live test on 22 Oct 2026 got **206 Partial Content** for both a head range and a mid-file range, with `access-control-allow-origin: *`, and a CORS preflight for the `Range` header from `https://selects.ikpemosi.work` succeeded on both `huggingface.co` and the `us.aws.cdn.hf.co` byte host (research note `architecture_delivery.md`). An older report of broken preflight concerned the retired `cas-bridge.xethub.hf.co` host ([datasets #7931](https://github.com/huggingface/datasets/issues/7931)) and no longer applies. transformers.js 4.3.1 exposes `env.fetch` and `env.customCache` hooks, so a Range loader can sit underneath it: resolve the redirect once, fetch 4 to 8 MB pieces with 2 or 3 in flight, retry each with backoff, keep finished pieces across visits, check the total against `x-linked-size`, then hand transformers.js one assembled file. Pieces of that size matter because the bytes come from AWS Paris with about 0.9 s to first byte, against 0.15 s from Cloudflare's Lagos colo (live, desktop).

Compression is a smaller, secondary lever. Hugging Face sends model bytes uncompressed. In live 5 MB slices, the fp32 whisper encoder compressed to **0.54 with zstd or brotli** and the q8 decoder to about 0.44 to 0.49, while fp16 weights barely compressed (0.86 to 0.91); published work expects only about 17% savings on fp32 models ([IBM ZipNN](https://research.ibm.com/blog/Zip-NN-AI-compression)), so the encoder result is likely an artefact of fp16 weights stored as fp32 and needs a whole-file check. Picking the q8 files gets most of that saving with no re-hosting. If a Lagos mirror is wanted, **Cloudflare R2 fails the zero-cost gate**: its own get-started page requires an R2 subscription through checkout ([R2 get started, 21 Apr 2026](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/get-started/)) and third-party guides report a card or PayPal requirement. Cloudflare Pages, by contrast, serves files up to 25 MiB and up to 20,000 files on the Free plan ([Pages limits, 5 Sep 2026](https://developers.cloudflare.com/pages/platform/limits/)), so 8 MB shards could come from the Lagos edge, with Hugging Face Range requests as fallback. Pages' bandwidth terms for large binaries were not checked. Measure before building it, because a resumable loader against Hugging Face may be enough.

Wi-Fi-only prefetch is only partly possible. The Network Information API and Background Fetch are Chromium-only, not Baseline ([MDN effectiveType](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/NetworkInformation/effectiveType); [MDN Background Fetch, 12 Sep 2026](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Background_Fetch_API)), so on Android Chrome the app can defer the judge download to Wi-Fi, or let Background Fetch carry it overnight inside the midnight to 5 AM night-data window, while on iPhone it must ask the user and fall back to a timed probe (a 1 MB Range request). The current `suggestMode` already suggests Light on iOS, on Data Saver, on 2G/3G, below 1.5 Mbps, or when the model would take over a minute; it should add that measured probe for Safari and treat a missing `AudioDecoder` as its own case.

Competitors offer no template for this. OpusClip, Vizard, Submagic and Descript ask for the whole video or a link the server fetches, and their help pages say nothing about slow connections or audio-only upload; Descript recommends at least 10 Mbps upload ([Descript help](https://help.descript.com/hc/en-us/articles/34610427613325-Why-Is-My-Import-or-Upload-Taking-So-Long)) and in December 2025 closed a six-year-old request for "don't upload the original" because it had moved to full cloud storage ([Descript feedback board](https://feedback.descript.com/feature-requests/p/option-to-not-upload-original-file-to-the-cloud)). Sending about 4 MB of speech and 10 kB of text, never the video, is a genuine difference for Lagos users.

## Phones can render clips, but HEVC and Safari 27 need guards

The video stays on the device in every mode, so the phone's codecs are the product's limits. Selects uses mediabunny on WebCodecs with no ffmpeg.wasm fallback (repo `src/browser/package.json`). H.264 High `avc1.640028` encodes on **96.5% of Chrome Android sessions and 99.98% of Safari iOS** sessions ([avc1.640028](https://webcodecsfundamentals.org/codecs/avc1.640028.html), read 22 Oct 2026). Safari iOS decodes 10-bit HEVC Main 10 (the class of an iPhone 4K HDR recording) in **97.7%** of sessions, but Chrome Android only in **52.4%** ([hev1.2.4.L153.B0](https://webcodecsfundamentals.org/codecs/hev1.2.4.L153.B0.html)), because Chrome decodes HEVC only in hardware and chips such as the Helio G99 stop at 2K30 ([MediaTek Helio G99](https://www.mediatek.com/products/smartphones/mediatek-helio-g99)). An iPhone video sent to an Android phone is therefore a coin flip, and the decode check should run before transcription so the user hears early. On iOS, picking from Photos makes WebKit transcode the video to H.264 behind a "Preparing..." screen before the page receives it ([WebKit commit, 5 Mar 2024](https://www.mail-archive.com/webkit-changes@lists.webkit.org/msg211341.html)), which costs time proportional to the file and is invisible to the app; reports conflict on whether `accept="video/quicktime"` avoids it, so time it on a device.

Render speed on phones is almost unmeasured. The one public benchmark gives 1080p30 H.264 encode at **12 fps in Safari on an iPhone 16 Pro (120 fps in Chrome on the same phone) and 200 fps in Chrome on a Galaxy S25** (undated, [webcodecsfundamentals VideoEncoder](https://webcodecsfundamentals.org/basics/encoder/)); at 12 fps a 45 s clip takes about 112 s. Selects rendered a 23.9 s 720x1280 preview in 7.8 to 9.6 s on a 2017 laptop (desktop, `notes/timings.md`), which is a floor. Two bugs need guards. Safari 27.0 and 27.0.1 hang forever on H.264 High or Main in the default "quality" latency mode ([mediabunny #541, Sep to Oct 2026](https://github.com/Vanilagy/mediabunny/issues/541)), which is exactly Selects' render configuration; the fix pattern is an 8-frame probe with a Baseline or realtime fallback plus a watchdog ([hyperaudio-lite-editor #721, 10 Oct 2026](https://github.com/hyperaudio/hyperaudio-lite-editor/issues/721)). And canvas `filter` is disabled in Safari 18.0 to 26.3 ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_canvasrenderingcontext2d_filter)), so the blurred "fit" background renders sharp on iPhones unless the blur is done by downscaling. Memory is not the render's problem (a 3-frame decode pool and a 20 to 45 MB output buffer); it is models plus long-file audio inside the 1.5 GB iPhone tab budget, so one model at a time and unload before rendering. No public measurement of battery or thermal cost exists for WebCodecs or WASM inference on phones; hardware encode should be cheap, sustained whisper on a Helio phone will throttle. The defaults that follow are 720x1280 at 30 fps, one clip at a time, top three first, and 1080x1920 only after a short on-device encode probe shows about 30 fps.

## Ranked design, expected numbers, risks and the test plan

### Recommendation, in order

**1. Light mode as the default whenever the speech model is not already cached (shipped; make it progressive).** Keep the shipped core: on-device decode, exact-offset pieces (30 s first, then up to 2 minutes), Opus 16 kbps with AAC, MP3 and WAV fallbacks, Groq whisper-large-v3 through the proxy with Cloudflare Workers AI turbo as failover on 429 or 5xx, and the optional personal Groq key. Add, in priority order: start sending pieces during decode; produce a preliminary shortlist and preview from the first 3 to 5 minutes; draw captions in the page for review and render only on approval; a `decodeAudioData` fallback for Safari before 26; a vocabulary prompt; and a clear daily-quota message with the own-key path when both free tiers return 429.

**2. Private mode as an opt-in, made reliable.** Replace both download paths (transformers.js via `env.fetch`/`env.customCache`, and `cache.js` for the judge) with a Range-resumable piece loader; switch the default speech set to an all-q8 base (76.9 MB, multilingual if it wins the accent test) with tiny.en q8 (40.8 MB) as the slow-link set, after a WER check; show the model-free preliminary ranking first so no judge download blocks the first clip; fetch the judge only on request, on Wi-Fi, or overnight via Background Fetch on Android; on WebKit use the plain WASM bundle, consider `COEP: require-corp` for threads, and call `navigator.storage.persist()` after a successful download.

**3. Automatic choice by measurement.** Choose Private only when the speech model is cached or a probe measures at least about 10 Mbps on a non-metered link and the device has at least 4 GB (Chromium `deviceMemory`) or is an iPhone on iOS 26+; otherwise Light. Without `AudioDecoder`, use the fallback decode or say plainly that the browser is too old.

**Rejected:** Web Speech API, Moonshine (no word times on Selects' runtime), Parakeet, Canary, Kyutai, distil-whisper, Vosk, Hugging Face Inference Providers, Fireworks, Replicate, DeepInfra, Render self-hosting, and Cloudflare R2.

### Risks

Shared free quotas are the first: 8 Groq hours plus about 3.6 Cloudflare hours a day across all users, 20 requests a minute on Groq, and the free Render proxy's 15 to 60 s wake (14.4 s observed), which the page-load wake call hides only if the user lingers. Privacy is the second: Light mode sends speech audio to Groq in the US (no retention by default, abuse logs up to 30 days), which the Light copy states and which some users will decline. Third, Cloudflare's signup and Free-plan cap behaviour (error rather than billing) are inferred from documentation, not observed. Fourth, accuracy: hosted large-v3 still misses about a quarter of words on strong Nigerian accents, and the q8 on-device sets have no accent measurement at all. Fifth, iOS: pre-26 Safari cannot decode audio, Safari 27 can hang the encoder, Photos transcoding adds unmeasured minutes, and storage eviction can delete a model after seven days. Sixth, every phone timing in this report is an estimate.

### What Arnold must sign up for

Two free accounts, both without a card: a **Groq** account at console.groq.com, whose API key goes into the Render proxy as `GROQ_API_KEY` (if the builders have not already done so), and a **Cloudflare** account with a Workers AI API token and account ID for `CF_API_TOKEN` and `CF_ACCOUNT_ID`, kept on the Free plan: never enable Workers Paid or R2. ## Conclusion

The iPhone failure looked like a model-size problem, but it was two problems: a downloader that cannot resume, and a pipeline that puts a download on the critical path at all. Removing the download (Light mode) beats shrinking it, because the uplink in Lagos is narrow but a quarter-megabyte request survives where a 140 MB stream does not, and the hosted large-v3 model is more accurate on Nigerian English than anything a phone can carry. The next gain is not network work at all: it is letting the first clip appear after the first few minutes of speech instead of after the whole transcript.

The zero-cost gate turns out to shape scale more than speed. Free tiers cover early users comfortably, but the shared daily quota of about 11.6 hours of speech (8 on Groq, 3.6 on Cloudflare) is the ceiling to watch, and the per-user Groq key is the free, card-less release valve. Private mode remains the privacy promise, and it should be described honestly as the slower path on Nigerian mobile data until real phone measurements say otherwise.
